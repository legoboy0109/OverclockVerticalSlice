## Overview

The **AI Opponent** is the computer-controlled rival commander that plays the full tempo-duel economy against the human player in skirmish missions — the same build/produce/move/attack/research triage a human plays, but decided by a heuristic instead of a person. Technically, it is a decision layer, not a new game system: it reads the same render-decoupled, cloneable game state every other system already exposes (`clone()`, `entities()`, `current_ap()`), evaluates its options through the identical side-effect-free preview queries the player's own interface uses (`reachable()`, `legal_targets()`, `preview_damage()`, `can_afford()`), and commits its choices through the same atomic `apply_action()` every player action routes through. There is no AI-only shortcut, no hidden currency, and no privileged information — the AI plays by the same rules it can see. For the Vertical Slice, this system deliberately targets a **simple, tempo-competent heuristic** (the concept prototype already proved a greedy AI is playable and gives "a real race") rather than deep search or learned play — matching systems-index's guidance to "ship a simple greedy heuristic first, deepen later." Without this system, the vertical slice's tempo duel has no opponent outside hot-seat, and Pillar 2 (*Tempo Is the Skill*) has nothing to be tested against.

## Player Fantasy

> *Lean review mode: `creative-director` not consulted for this section — draft written directly from the game concept's Core Fantasy and Pillar 2. Flag for a pass before production if the AI's feel diverges from this in playtesting.*

**The feeling: facing a rival who is actually racing you.**

The Core Fantasy is "mastery of tempo" against a rival commander who reads the board and times their swing — and that fantasy is only real if the opponent genuinely contests tempo back. The AI Opponent's job is to be a **credible sparring partner**, not a puzzle and not a pushover: it should convert an AP advantage when it has one, punish an overextension when the player leaves one open, and mount a real push rather than turtling passively. When the player pulls ahead, it should feel like they *out-raced* something that was actually racing — not that they walked past an obstacle. When the player falls behind, the AI's pressure should be legible in hindsight: "I see what they were doing" (a boom into a Production Outpost, a rush timed to hit before the player's spike), never a move that reads as arbitrary or scripted.

Two failure modes this system must design against, because both quietly destroy the tempo-duel fantasy: an AI that plays too passively (never really contests tempo, so victory feels unearned) and an AI that plays with information the player couldn't see on the visible board (since Grid & Terrain and Combat Resolution are both fully deterministic and unfogged, the AI reasons over exactly the same board state the player does — it must never "know" a value or read a bluff it shouldn't be able to see, or the perfect-information trust the whole game is built on breaks).

For the Vertical Slice, "credible" does not mean "masterful." A simple heuristic that reliably budgets its AP, expands its economy, and presses when it has an edge already delivers most of this fantasy — the concept prototype's own greedy AI proved a real race is achievable without deep search. The goal is a rival that keeps the duel honest, not one that's unbeatable.

## Detailed Design

> *Lean review mode: `game-designer`/`systems-designer` not consulted for this section — drafted directly from the dependency digest and Player Fantasy above. Flag for a pass before production, especially the scoring model in CR-3.*

### Core Rules

**CR-1 — Turn scope.** The AI acts only during its own Action phase, exactly as a human player does — it is a second `active_player` from Game State & Turn Manager's point of view, with no privileged phase access. It never acts on the opponent's turn or during Start-of-turn/End-of-turn resolution.

**CR-2 — The decision loop.** Each AI turn is a repeating **evaluate → commit** loop, not a single planned sequence: (1) clone the authoritative game state via `clone()`; (2) enumerate every currently-legal, currently-affordable action across every verb the active entities can perform this instant (move, attack, produce, build, research, cancel-build); (3) score each candidate (CR-3); (4) commit the highest-scoring candidate via the real `apply_action()` (not the clone); (5) repeat from (1) — re-cloning fresh, since the board just changed. The loop ends when no candidate scores above the pass threshold (Tuning Knobs) or AP will not cover any remaining legal action, whichever comes first, then the AI ends its turn.

**CR-3 — One scoring scale, all verbs.** Every candidate action, regardless of verb, is scored on the same normalized scale (see Formulas) so a move, an attack, a build, and a research start are genuinely compared against each other for "best use of AP right now" — mirroring the human triage this game is built around (Pillar 1). The AI never runs verb-siloed logic (e.g., "always produce before moving") that would substitute a fixed priority order for real evaluation.

**CR-4 — No privileged information, no privileged cost.** The AI calls the exact same side-effect-free queries the player's interface calls (`reachable()`, `legal_targets()`, `preview_damage()`, `can_afford()`, `legal_build_tiles()`, `legal_deploy_tiles()`, `completed_outpost_count()`) and pays the exact same AP costs. It has no bonus income, no discount, no fog-breaking read of enemy intent, and no access to any value not derivable from the current visible board state — Grid & Terrain and Combat Resolution are both fully deterministic and unfogged, so "visible board state" already includes everything, but the AI must not read anything *beyond* it. In particular, the human player's un-committed **preview/hover state** (the highlighted move range or damage readout the player is *considering* before pressing confirm) is a UI-layer artifact in Command & Action Interface — it never exists in the render-decoupled game state the AI clones, so there is nothing there for the AI to read even in principle. This isolation is asserted here as a rule and verified by AC-6b, not merely assumed.

**CR-5 — AP is spent, not hoarded.** Because unspent AP is discarded at end-of-turn (`ap_reset_policy`, no banking), the loop in CR-2 does not stop just because the *best-scoring* action is expensive — it continues finding the best legal use of *remaining* AP until nothing scores above the pass threshold. A turn that ends with a large AP surplus and legal, positive-scoring actions still available is a defect in the scoring/threshold tuning, not an acceptable outcome. **The one intentional throttle** is `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN` (the dominant-strategy cadence guardrail — Formulas): once the AI has committed that many economy-outpost + research investments this turn, further economy/research candidates are dropped from CR-2's enumerated pool and remaining AP flows to the best non-economy actions (or is passed). This bounds a single-turn runaway boom **without** hoarding AP or understating any single investment's value.

**CR-6 — Guaranteed termination.** The loop in CR-2 always terminates: each successful commit either spends AP (shrinking the affordable-action set) or the candidate set is empty. The loop terminates on any of three conditions: no legal action exists, every candidate scores at or below the pass threshold, or the commit's own win-check sets `match_status = GameOver` (the AI's own lethal action ended the match). In every case the AI ends its turn immediately — there is no retry, no stall, and no possibility of a hung turn.

**CR-7 — Reactive-safety exception (contested-threat check).** A **finishing/immediately-lethal** attack — one that removes a nearly-dead enemy unit's or structure's last hp this instant — must not be deprioritized into a generic economy score. It is guaranteed priority by a fixed **floor bonus** applied *within* `action_score` as a post-hoc `max(base_score, LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS)` (see the `action_score` formula), **not** by a pre-filter that short-circuits enumeration. This ordering matters: every candidate (lethal and non-lethal) is still enumerated and `base_score`-scored normally, and the floor is applied to lethal candidates *after* scoring — so two competing lethal attacks are still correctly compared by the Edge-Cases tie-break (lowest `ap_cost`, then lowest entity ID) rather than "first lethal found wins." It is a scoring-weight rule that composes with CR-3, it does not bypass it.

### States and Transitions

The AI's turn is a small loop, not a rich FSM (it has no player input to react to):

| From state | Trigger | To state | Side effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TURN_START** | Game State & Turn Manager hands the AI `active_player` | EVALUATING | `clone()` the authoritative state |
| **EVALUATING** | Candidate set enumerated | SCORING | Enumerate every legal+affordable action across all owned entities/verbs (CR-2 step 2) |
| **SCORING** | Candidates scored | (branch) | Score every candidate on the common scale (CR-3, CR-7) |
| SCORING | Best candidate score > pass threshold | COMMITTING | — |
| SCORING | No candidate scores above pass threshold, or none affordable | TURN_END | — |
| **COMMITTING** | Commit best candidate | EVALUATING | Real `apply_action()` on authoritative state (not the clone); board changes |
| COMMITTING | `apply_action()` rejects (state changed between clone and commit — see Edge Cases) | EVALUATING | Discard the stale clone, re-loop from a fresh `clone()`; no AP spent |
| COMMITTING | The commit's own win-check sets `match_status = GameOver` | **TURN_END** *(terminal)* | The AI's own lethal action ended the match; the loop stops immediately — no further clone/evaluate/commit |
| **TURN_END** | — | (turn handoff) | AI calls End Turn exactly as a human player would; Game State & Turn Manager advances to the opponent |

### Interactions with Other Systems

This system **owns**: the evaluation/scoring loop and the decision of *which* legal action to take. It **never** owns: cost math, damage math, legality rules, pathfinding, or AP deduction — identical boundary to Command & Action Interface, and for the same reason (Pass-Through Invariant-style discipline: the AI holds no copy of any balance constant it can read live).

| System | Queries this AI calls | Commits this AI calls | What this AI does with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Game State & Turn Manager** (#2) | `clone()`, `active_player`, `current_ap(player)`, `entities()`, `entity_at`, `match_status` | `apply_action()` (all verbs route through this), End Turn | The AI's entire read/write surface — `clone()` is the designated lookahead mechanism (named for this purpose in that GDD) |
| **Movement** (#5) | `reachable(unit) → {tile, min_cost, is_surcharged}` | `move(unit, dest)` (via `apply_action`) | Candidate move tiles + exact AP cost per candidate |
| **Combat Resolution** (#6) | `legal_targets(unit)`, `legal_targets(unit, from_tile)`, `preview_damage(atk, tgt)` | `attack(atk, tgt)` (via `apply_action`) | Candidate targets, exact damage, and — via the hypothetical-tile overload — move+attack combos in one scoring pass |
| **Base & Production** (#7) | `legal_build_tiles`, `legal_deploy_tiles`, `completed_outpost_count` | `build()`, `produce()`, `cancel_build()` (via `apply_action`) | Candidate structures/units and where to place them |
| **Research/Tech** (#8) | `legal_research_targets(lab)` | `start_research()`, `cancel_research()` (via `apply_action`) | Candidate tech starts |
| **AP Economy** (#3) | `can_afford(player, amount)`, `current_ap(player)`, `income(player)` | *(none — AP is spent inside each system's commit)* | The affordability gate every candidate is filtered through before scoring (CR-4) |
| **Grid & Terrain** (#1) | `manhattan_distance`, `terrain_at`, `occupant_at` (indirectly, via the above queries) | — | Positional inputs to scoring (e.g., distance-to-HQ threat evaluation) |

**Downstream dependents:** None yet. Faction Identity (#12, designed last) may eventually give the AI per-faction weighting differences, but that is out of scope for this GDD.

**Ownership note:** the AI is a *consumer*, not an owner, of every formula and rule it reasons about — matching the "no privileged information, no privileged cost" principle in CR-4.

## Formulas

> *Lean review mode: drafted by `systems-designer` with locked numeric guidance from `economy-designer` (HP_PER_AP, kill-denial term, decay+cap ROI shape for economy/research). Flag for a pass before production.*

All four formulas below share one job: convert every legal candidate action — regardless of verb — into a single comparable number, satisfying CR-3. The model has three layers:

1. **`ap_equivalent_value(action)`** — a verb-dispatched formula that converts an action's board effect into an abstract "AP-equivalent value" (the same unit AP itself is measured in). Each verb has its own conversion sub-formula (`combat_value`, `production_value`, `economy_value`, `research_value`), but all four output into this one shared unit.
2. **`action_score(action)`** — divides `ap_equivalent_value` by the action's real AP cost to get a density/ROI ratio, then applies CR-7's fixed lethal-floor bonus as a post-hoc `max()` floor override (applied *after* scoring, not a pre-filter — see CR-7). This is the number the evaluate→commit loop (CR-2) actually compares across every candidate.
3. **`PASS_THRESHOLD`** — the minimum `action_score` below which the AI stops taking actions and ends its turn (closes the loop per CR-2/CR-6).

**Dominant-strategy guardrail note (decoupled from valuation — 2026-07-22 revision):** AP Economy (#3) and Research/Tech (#8) carried an Economy-Tech dominant-strategy risk (boom + large-map, uncapped income compounding), now mitigated at the base-game level by pinning the Vertical Slice board to a fixed 14×16 (grid-terrain.md), and both are back to **Approved**. This GDD deliberately does **not** guard that risk by capping the AI's *valuation* of economy/research. An earlier draft did — a flat `ROI_CAP_MULTIPLIER` on `economy_value`/`research_value` — but that (a) made the AI value its own economy ~35% below what its own horizon/decay model says it is worth, (b) because the cap *always* bound, flattened every Economy Outpost to an identical score so the AI could not tell a great first outpost from a marginal seventh, and (c) made the AI a systematically worse economic player than an uncapped human, who would reliably out-boom it. The two concerns are now separated: `economy_value`/`research_value` value investments **honestly** (uncapped decayed projection, so the AI can rank them), and the dominant-strategy risk is guarded — **primarily** by the base-game 14×16 board pin + tile scarcity + `CLOSEOUT_TARGET_TURNS`, and **secondarily** by a cadence limit, `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN` (Tuning Knobs), which bounds how many economy/research commits the AI makes in a single turn so it cannot spam a runaway boom in one pass without lying to its own valuation. Note (per the 2026-07-22 economy-designer re-review) the cadence cap is a *back-up*, not the load-bearing guard on the pinned board — it does not meaningfully bind until ~turn 3–4; see OQ-5. If a future retune re-opens the underlying economy risk, tighten the cadence limit (not the valuation) **and** re-derive it against a turn-by-turn model. **Uncapping `economy_value` has one downstream invariant to respect:** its ceiling now scales with `ECONOMY_HORIZON`/`ECONOMY_DECAY`, and `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` must stay above that ceiling for CR-7 to hold — see the `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` rows and the Knob Interactions note.

### `combat_value` — AP-equivalent value of an attack

**Expression:**

```
combat_value(attacker, target) =
    ap_cost_opponent_paid_for(target) × (hp_removed / target_max_hp)
    + (is_kill ? KILL_DENIAL_RATE × ap_cost_opponent_paid_for(target) : 0)
```

where `hp_removed = min(preview_damage(attacker, target), target.hp)` and `is_kill = (hp_removed >= target.hp)`.

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `attacker` | entity ref | any owned unit/Defensive Structure | The candidate attacker |
| `target` | entity ref | any legal enemy target | The candidate target (`legal_targets`/`legal_targets(from_tile)`) |
| `ap_cost_opponent_paid_for(target)` | int | 2–12 | The opponent's sunk AP cost for the target, **read live from the entity's own field** (never a memorized table — so it can never silently drift from a roster/cost retune): `produce_cost` for units (2/4/7/5), `build_cost` for buildable structures (4/9/6/8). **The HQ is the sole exception** — it is placed at setup with no `build_cost` (see base-production.md structure table), so it uses the dedicated `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE` constant (default 12, Tuning Knobs) instead. `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE` is **not** a sunk-cost figure (the HQ was never paid for in AP) — it is a deliberately-chosen siege-priority weight reflecting that the HQ **is the win condition**, set at or above the most expensive structure (Production Outpost, 9) so a legitimate siege never scores below chipping an ordinary structure |
| `hp_removed` | int | 1–10 | `preview_damage(attacker, target)` clamped to the target's remaining hp (Combat's pure query; MIN_DAMAGE floor already applied) |
| `target_max_hp` | int | 3–40 | The target's full (undamaged) hp stat |
| `is_kill` | bool | {true, false} | True if this attack would remove the target's last hp |
| `KILL_DENIAL_RATE` | float | 0.3–0.7 (default 0.5) | Fraction of the victim's sunk AP cost credited as bonus value for denying it outright, on top of the prorated hp-damage term |
| `combat_value` | float | 0 – ~13.5 | AP-equivalent value of this attack (unbounded upward in theory; practically capped by roster stats + `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE` — see worked examples) |

**Output range:** `[0, ~13.5]`. Floor is 0 only if `hp_removed` is 0, which cannot happen given Combat's `MIN_DAMAGE=1` floor on any legal, landed attack — so in practice this is always positive for **any** legal target, **including the HQ** (with `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE` defined, a non-lethal HQ chip is `12 × (hp_removed/40) > 0`, never the degenerate 0 an undefined term would have produced). No hard ceiling is applied (unlike `economy_value`/`research_value`) because a single attack cannot compound the way banked future income can; the natural ceiling is bounded by the roster's own `produce_cost`/`build_cost` values plus `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE` (max opponent-paid weight 12 for the HQ, but the HQ's 40 hp means no single hit removes a large fraction, so its per-hit `combat_value` stays modest — practically ≤ ~8; the true one-shot worst-case around 13.5 is still a full-hp kill on a Production Outpost: `9 × (14/14) + 0.5×9 = 13.5`).

**Worked example 1 — non-lethal trade:** A Trooper (atk 3) attacks an enemy Trooper (hp 6, `produce_cost` 4) at full health, no cover, no research. `preview_damage` = `max(1, 3-0-0) = 3`. `hp_removed = min(3, 6) = 3`. `target_max_hp = 6`. `is_kill = (3 >= 6) = false`.
`combat_value = 4 × (3/6) + 0 = 2.0`.

**Worked example 2 — finishing blow (same attack, but the target already took prior damage down to 3 hp remaining):** `preview_damage = 3`, `hp_removed = min(3, 3) = 3`, `target_max_hp = 6` (still the target's *full* hp — the denominator never changes), `is_kill = (3 >= 3) = true`.
`combat_value = 4 × (3/6) + 0.5 × 4 = 2.0 + 2.0 = 4.0`. The kill-denial term exactly doubles the value of an otherwise-identical 3-damage hit, because it also denies the opponent's remaining sunk cost in that Trooper — this is the general-case mechanism that composes with CR-7's floor bonus rather than duplicating it (CR-7 additionally guarantees this specific action is never *outscored* by a marginal economy play, even if the decay numbers above happened to produce a lower score in some edge case).

**Worked example 3 — non-lethal HQ siege (the win-condition target):** A Sniper (atk 6, +1 Attack Tech = effective 7) attacks the enemy HQ (hp 40, `defense` 2, structures are cover-immune). `preview_damage = max(1, 7 − 0 − 2) = 5`. `hp_removed = min(5, 40) = 5`, `target_max_hp = 40`, `is_kill = false`. Using `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE = 12` for `ap_cost_opponent_paid_for(HQ)`:
`combat_value = 12 × (5/40) + 0 = 1.5`. `action_score = 1.5 / 2 = 0.75` — comfortably above `PASS_THRESHOLD` (0.15), so the AI **will** voluntarily chip the HQ down across the multi-turn siege Combat Resolution's design relies on, rather than ignoring it until a one-shot kill is available. *(Before `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE` was defined, `ap_cost_opponent_paid_for(HQ)` was undefined → a literal implementation scored every such attack at 0, below threshold, and the AI would never press the win condition. This example is the fix's regression guard.)*

### `production_value` — AP-equivalent value of producing a unit

**Expression:**

```
production_value(unit_type, deploy_tile) =
    produce_cost(unit_type) × REACHABILITY_MULTIPLIER(unit_type, deploy_tile)
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `unit_type` | enum | {scout, trooper, heavy, sniper} | The candidate unit to produce |
| `deploy_tile` | tile ref | any `legal_deploy_tile` | Where the new unit would be placed |
| `produce_cost(unit_type)` | int | 2–7 | The unit's own registered cost (Scout 2, Trooper 4, Heavy 7, Sniper 5) — a produced unit is a stored-AP asset, so its value anchors directly to this, not a separately-invented "power score" |
| `REACHABILITY_MULTIPLIER` | float | {0.9, 1.0, 1.1} | Small board-context adjustment: **1.1** if the unit, from `deploy_tile`, is within `attack_range + soft_move_cap` reach of at least one live enemy unit or structure in this turn's clone (can contribute to the active fight immediately); **1.0** if it cannot reach any enemy yet but the two sides are **in contact** — *operationally defined* as: some friendly entity and some enemy entity are each within the other's `attack_range + soft_move_cap` reach (the sides can engage next turn); **0.9** if no side is in contact (isolated / opening-turn production). This three-way test is deterministic and constructable by a tester from board state alone — no vague "active fight" judgment. |
| `production_value` | float | 1.8 – 7.7 | AP-equivalent value of producing this unit |

**Output range:** `[1.8, 7.7]` — floor is Scout at the 0.9 multiplier (2×0.9=1.8), ceiling is Heavy at the 1.1 multiplier (7×1.1=7.7).

**Worked example:** Producing a Trooper (`produce_cost` 4) from a Production Outpost, where the deploy tile is within reach of an enemy unit visible on the board this turn: `REACHABILITY_MULTIPLIER = 1.1`. `production_value = 4 × 1.1 = 4.4`. Cost to produce = 4 AP. `action_score` (below) = 4.4/4 = 1.1.

### `economy_value` — AP-equivalent value of building an Economy Outpost

**Expression:**

```
economy_value(build_action) =
    raw_immediate_value + Σ_{t=1}^{ECONOMY_HORIZON} marginal_ap_income(t) × ECONOMY_DECAY^t
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `raw_immediate_value` | float | fixed 0 | An Economy Outpost has `build_time = 1`, so it produces no immediate board effect the turn it's started — always 0 for this action type |
| `ECONOMY_HORIZON` | int | fixed 6 | Number of future turns of projected income counted toward this build's value |
| `marginal_ap_income(t)` | float | 0–3 | The `ap_income` gain this outpost contributes at future turn `t`, per AP Economy's tiered formula (`OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER1`=2 for this player's outposts 1–4, `OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER2`=1 for 5+, plus `ECONOMY_TECH_INCOME_BONUS`=1 if the player already has Economy Tech and this outpost is within `ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD`) |
| `ECONOMY_DECAY` | float | fixed 0.85 | Per-turn discount applied to future income — later turns count for progressively less, reflecting that AP realized sooner is worth more in a tempo race |
| `economy_value` | float | 0 – ~7.1 | AP-equivalent value of this build — now *varies* with the outpost's actual marginal income (no cap), so the AI can rank a strong buy above a weak one (see worked example) |

**Output range:** `[0, ~7.1]` in the VS. `economy_value` now varies with the outpost's actual marginal income: a first tier-1 outpost (+2 AP/turn) evaluates to ≈7.06; a tier-2 outpost past `TIER_THRESHOLD` (+1 AP/turn) to roughly half that; an outpost past `ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD` (no further Economy-Tech bonus) lower still. This differentiation is the whole point of decoupling the guardrail from the valuation — the AI can now rank a great economy buy above a marginal one, which the old always-binding cap made impossible.

**Worked example:** A player with 0 completed Economy Outposts builds their first one (no Economy Tech yet). Every future turn `t=1..6` this outpost contributes the flat tier-1 marginal `+2` AP/turn (`OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER1`, since it's outpost #1 of ≤4). `raw_immediate_value = 0`.

`Σ_{t=1}^{6} 2 × 0.85^t = 2 × (0.85+0.7225+0.614125+0.522006+0.443705+0.377150) = 2 × 3.529486 = 7.058973`.

`economy_value ≈ 7.06`. `action_score = 7.06 / 4 = 1.765`. Contrast a **seventh** outpost (tier-2, +1 AP/turn, `OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER2`): `Σ_{t=1}^{6} 1 × 0.85^t = 3.529`, `economy_value ≈ 3.53`, `action_score = 3.53/4 = 0.88`. The AI now correctly values outpost #1 nearly 2× outpost #7 — instead of scoring both an identical 1.15 as the old cap forced. Note `action_score` 1.765 still sits well below `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` (3.5), so honest economy valuation does **not** let a boom outscore a finishing blow — the invariant CR-7 depends on still holds (the roster's highest ordinary non-lethal `action_score` is now this ~1.77, comfortably under the floor).

### `research_value` — AP-equivalent value of starting a tech

**Expression:**

```
research_value(tech) =
    Σ_{t=research_time(tech)+1}^{HORIZON(tech)} marginal_tech_value(tech, t) × ECONOMY_DECAY^t

where HORIZON(tech) = TECH_VALUE_HORIZON  for Attack Tech and Defense Tech (permanent army-wide buffs)
      HORIZON(tech) = ECONOMY_HORIZON     for Economy Tech (an income projection)
```

Reuses `ECONOMY_DECAY` from `economy_value` (one shared per-turn discount curve). The sum starts *after* `research_time` turns have elapsed — the tech's bonus contributes nothing to `marginal_tech_value` before it completes. **The horizon differs by tech type.** Attack and Defense Tech are *permanent, army-wide* buffs that pay out for the entire remaining game, so they use the longer `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` (default 10); the 6-turn `ECONOMY_HORIZON` truncated their payoff so aggressively that Defense Tech (whose longer `research_time` of 4 leaves only *two* counted turns inside a 6-turn window) scored *below* `PASS_THRESHOLD` and would never have been researched at all. Economy Tech is an income *projection*, so it stays on `ECONOMY_HORIZON` (6), consistent with `economy_value`. **No valuation cap is applied** (see the decoupling note above) — the dominant-strategy risk is guarded by `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN`, not by understating a tech's worth.

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `tech` | enum | {attack_tech, defense_tech, economy_tech} | The candidate tech to start |
| `research_time(tech)` | int | 3–4 | Turns before the tech's effect goes live (Attack 3, Defense 4, Economy 3) — delay-shifts the summation's start index |
| `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` | int | fixed 10 | Horizon for **permanent** army-wide buffs (Attack, Defense) — longer than `ECONOMY_HORIZON` because a permanent stat buff keeps paying for the whole remaining game, not just a 6-turn window |
| `marginal_tech_value(tech, t)` | float | 0–6 | Per-turn AP-equivalent value once live. For Attack/Defense Tech: `(RESEARCH_ATK_BONUS or DEFENSE_TECH_BONUS) / HP_PER_AP × ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE`. For Economy Tech: `ECONOMY_TECH_INCOME_BONUS × min(projected_completed_outposts, ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD)`, reusing AP Economy's income model — where `projected_completed_outposts` = the player's completed **plus under-construction** Economy Outposts (so researching Economy Tech *while a boom is in flight*, the sequencing base-production.md calls the better play, is not wrongly scored at 0) |
| `HP_PER_AP` | float | fixed 1.5 | Global hp-to-AP exchange rate (also used by `combat_value` via `preview_damage` economics) — anchored to the roster's best hp/AP unit efficiency (Trooper, Scout at 1.5) |
| `ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE` | float | 0.5–2 (default 1.5) | A stated, auditable assumption for how many attacks this player lands per turn on average — converts a flat attack-stat buff into a per-turn AP-equivalent rate. **Set to a defensible mid-range 1.5** ("about three attacks every two turns"), *not* reverse-engineered to clear a threshold (the earlier 1.8 was — see the revised tuning flag below). Still needs validation against real playtest attack-frequency data (OQ-2) |
| `projected_completed_outposts` | int | 0–~12 | The Economy-Tech-researching player's completed **+ under-construction** Economy Outposts, read live — the `n` fed to Economy Tech's `marginal_tech_value` |
| `research_cost(tech)` | int | 7–10 | Attack/Defense 10, Economy 7 |
| `research_value` | float | 0 – ~2.4 (Attack), 0 – ~1.85 (Defense), 0 – ~8.06 (Economy) | AP-equivalent value of starting this tech (uncapped honest projection; no `ROI_CAP_MULTIPLIER`) |

**Output range:** Attack `[0, ~2.4]`, Defense `[0, ~1.85]`, Economy `[0, ~8.06]` (the Economy ceiling is the `projected_completed_outposts = ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD = 6` case: `1×6 × Σ_{t=4}^{6}0.85^t = 6 × 1.342861 ≈ 8.06`). No valuation cap — the dominant-strategy risk is guarded by `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN`, not by understating each investment's worth. See the decoupling note above.

**Worked example — Attack Tech:** `research_time = 3`, `research_cost = 10`, `HORIZON = TECH_VALUE_HORIZON = 10`. `marginal_tech_value = RESEARCH_ATK_BONUS / HP_PER_AP × ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE = 1 / 1.5 × 1.5 = 1.0` for every `t` once live. The summation runs `t = 4` to `10` (7 turns — the buff is permanent, counted across the full tech horizon, not truncated at 6):

`Σ_{t=4}^{10} 1.0 × 0.85^t ≈ 2.3644`. `research_value = 2.3644`, `action_score = 2.3644 / 10 ≈ 0.236` — clears `PASS_THRESHOLD` (0.15) with a ~57% margin (no longer the knife-edge ~7% of the earlier draft).

**Worked example — Defense Tech (was the broken case):** `research_time = 4`, `research_cost = 10`, `HORIZON = 10`. `marginal_tech_value = DEFENSE_TECH_BONUS / HP_PER_AP × ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE = 1 / 1.5 × 1.5 = 1.0`. The summation runs `t = 5` to `10` (6 turns — one fewer than Attack Tech, because its `research_time` is one turn longer):

`Σ_{t=5}^{10} 1.0 × 0.85^t ≈ 1.8424`. `research_value = 1.8424`, `action_score = 1.8424 / 10 ≈ 0.184` — **now clears** `PASS_THRESHOLD`. *(Under the earlier draft's 6-turn `ECONOMY_HORIZON`, Defense Tech saw only `t = 5,6` = two counted turns and scored ≈0.0985, failing the threshold by ~34% — the AI would never have researched it, despite the doc claiming "Attack/Defense Tech clears the threshold." Extending permanent buffs to `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` fixes both techs honestly, by counting the buff's real permanent lifetime rather than fudging the per-turn constant to force a pass.)*

**Worked example — Economy Tech:** `research_time = 3`, `research_cost = 7`, `HORIZON = ECONOMY_HORIZON = 6`. `marginal_tech_value = ECONOMY_TECH_INCOME_BONUS × min(projected_completed_outposts, ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD)`. Suppose the AI has 2 completed Economy Outposts and 1 more under construction → `projected_completed_outposts = 3`, so `marginal = 1 × min(3,6) = 3` per turn. Summation `t = 4` to `6`:

`Σ_{t=4}^{6} 3 × 0.85^t = 3 × 1.342861 ≈ 4.029`. `action_score = 4.029 / 7 ≈ 0.576` — clears comfortably. **Critically**, because `projected_completed_outposts` counts outposts already **under construction**, an AI mid-boom that researches Economy Tech *before* its in-flight outposts finish is no longer scored at 0 — which the earlier `n`-at-evaluation-time formula wrongly did whenever the AI had 0 *completed* outposts, inverting the game's own recommended sequencing.

**Revised tuning flag (2026-07-22):** `ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE` is now `1.5` — a defensible mid-range assumption, no longer reverse-engineered to make a tech barely clear the threshold. The earlier draft set it to 1.8 (near the top of range) *specifically* so Attack Tech cleared `PASS_THRESHOLD` by ~7% — and Defense Tech still silently failed anyway. Moving permanent-buff payoff onto `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` (10) does the work honestly: both techs clear with healthy margins at a realistic attack rate. The estimate still needs validation against real playtest data (OQ-2), but it is no longer a value whose *only* justification was the pass/fail outcome it produced. If playtest shows the true rate is much lower, lower `PASS_THRESHOLD` toward its 0.05 floor rather than re-inflating this constant.

### `action_score` — the one common scale (CR-3)

**Expression:**

```
action_score(action) =
    is_immediately_lethal(action)
        ? max(base_score(action), LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS)
        : base_score(action)

where base_score(action) = ap_equivalent_value(action) / ap_cost(action)
```

`ap_equivalent_value(action)` dispatches to `combat_value`, `production_value`, `economy_value`, or `research_value` by the action's verb; `move`-only actions (no attack) and `cancel-build` use the two supplementary formulas below (positional value and refund value respectively — see Edge Cases for the full move-scoring treatment, since a move alone rarely has a standalone AP-equivalent value the way the four resourced verbs do).

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `action` | action ref | any candidate from CR-2's enumeration | The candidate action being scored this loop iteration |
| `ap_equivalent_value(action)` | float | 0 – ~13.5 | Verb-dispatched value from the four formulas above |
| `ap_cost(action)` | int | 1–10 | The action's real AP cost (`attack_cost`=2, `DEFENSIVE_ATTACK_COST`=1, `produce_cost`, `build_cost`, `research_cost`, or `move_path_cost`) |
| `is_immediately_lethal(action)` | bool | {true, false} | True only for an attack where `is_kill = true` on `combat_value`'s inner computation (CR-7's trigger condition) |
| `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` | float | 2.5–5 (default 3.5) | Fixed score floor guaranteeing a finishing blow is never outscored by a marginal economy play, per CR-7. Must stay above the highest ordinary non-lethal `action_score` the roster can produce — which, since `economy_value` is now **uncapped** (2026-07-22 decoupling), is the first-outpost economy build, whose `action_score` **ceiling scales with `ECONOMY_HORIZON` and `ECONOMY_DECAY`**: `economy_ceiling_score = OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER1 × Σ_{t=1}^{ECONOMY_HORIZON} ECONOMY_DECAY^t / economy_outpost.build_cost`. At defaults this is ≈1.77; at safe-range maxima (`ECONOMY_HORIZON`=10, `ECONOMY_DECAY`=0.95) it rises to ≈3.81. **Invariant: `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` must exceed `economy_ceiling_score` for the current `ECONOMY_HORIZON`/`ECONOMY_DECAY`** — if either of those knobs is raised, recompute the ceiling and raise `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` to stay above it, or CR-7 silently fails (a non-lethal build outscores a finishing blow). See the same-named row and the cross-knob note in Tuning Knobs |
| `action_score` | float | 0 – ~7 (uncapped upward in theory, bounded in practice by roster stats and `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS`) | The one shared number CR-2's commit step compares across every candidate, of every verb |

**Output range:** `[0, ~7]` in practice for the Vertical Slice roster — no formal ceiling is imposed. Ordinary play clusters between ~0.18 (a tech-research start) and ~2.0 (a clean kill, or a strong first-outpost boom at ~1.77), with `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` (3.5 default) sitting comfortably above that band so it is never accidentally beaten by a non-lethal action under default tuning. (Neither `economy_value` nor `research_value` is capped any more — the dominant-strategy risk they posed is now guarded by the `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN` cadence limit; see the decoupling note above.)

**Determinism & tie semantics.** `action_score` is real-valued (division, geometric decay), so ties are compared with a tolerance `SCORE_TIE_EPSILON` (Tuning Knobs), never a raw-float `==`. On a single build/platform the same inputs produce the same floats (GDScript evaluates a deterministic instruction sequence), so AC-3's "same ordered sequence of commits on re-run" holds **for the same machine/build** — it does not promise, and does not need, bit-identical scores across platforms or compiler flags. The one intentional *exact*-tie case (two lethal attacks floored to the same `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` constant) is exact by construction; every other near-tie falls to the epsilon comparison.

**Worked example — comparing all three verb families on the same turn (demonstrates CR-3):**

| Candidate | `ap_equivalent_value` | `ap_cost` | `action_score` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trooper attacks enemy Trooper (non-lethal, Example 1 above) | 2.0 | 2 | **1.00** |
| Trooper attacks enemy Trooper (finishing blow, Example 2 above) | 4.0 | 2 | **2.00** (below `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` 3.5, so the floor overrides: **3.50**) |
| Build Economy Outpost #1 (worked example above) | 7.06 (uncapped) | 4 | **1.765** |
| Produce Trooper, reachable this turn (worked example above) | 4.4 | 4 | **1.10** |
| Start Attack Tech (worked example above) | 2.3644 | 10 | **0.236** |

All five numbers sit on one scale and are directly comparable, satisfying CR-3: with these candidates available in the same evaluate→commit iteration, the AI commits the finishing blow first (3.50, floor-guaranteed), then re-clones and re-evaluates — at which point the Outpost build (1.765, now honestly valued rather than cap-flattened) beats both the Trooper production (1.10) and the non-lethal attack (1.00), and Attack Tech (0.236) is scored last of the affordable options but still clears `PASS_THRESHOLD`, consistent with the "balanced tempo-racer" directive: no verb wins by construction, only by the numbers in front of the AI that turn.

### `PASS_THRESHOLD` — loop termination constant

**Expression:**

```
loop continues while max(action_score(candidate) for candidate in legal_affordable_candidates) > PASS_THRESHOLD
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|--------|------|-------|-------------|
| `PASS_THRESHOLD` | float | 0.05–0.3 (default 0.15) | The minimum `action_score` a candidate must clear for the AI to take it. Below this, remaining AP is judged not worth spending and the turn ends (CR-2, CR-6) |

**Output range:** N/A (this is a comparison constant, not a computed value).

**Why 0.15:** it sits above `production_value`'s worst-case floor under a poor `REACHABILITY_MULTIPLIER` (Scout at 0.9× = 1.8 value / 2 cost = 0.9 — comfortably clears) and below the tech-research scores under the revised permanent-buff horizon (Attack ≈0.236, Defense ≈0.184) — so a research investment clears the bar with real margin instead of the earlier knife-edge, and CR-5 ("AP must not be hoarded") is upheld: any action scoring above 0.15 is judged worth taking rather than ending the turn early. If playtesting shows the AI ending turns with large unspent AP despite legal actions available, lower `PASS_THRESHOLD` first (0.05 floor); if the AI spends on clearly bad trades, raise it (0.3 ceiling) — but note that raising it much above ~0.18 begins to push Defense Tech (0.184) back below the line, so re-check the tech worked examples after any large increase.

## Edge Cases

- **If a unit's only available action is a move that doesn't enable an attack this turn** (no combo target reachable): it is scored by a two-term positional model. **A pure repositioning move has no resource-denominated value**, so — like `cancel_build` below — it is a *documented, scoped exception* to CR-3's value-per-AP scale: it is normalized by **tiles traversed**, not AP cost, so a heavier unit is not penalized out of ever advancing merely for costing more AP per tile. Formally, `action_score = positional_value / tiles_moved`, where `tiles_moved` is the number of tiles the move traverses (≥ 1), and

  `positional_value(move) = POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED × max(0, dist_before − dist_after) + (sets_up_attack_next_turn(dest) ? SETUP_ADVANCE_BONUS : 0)`

  - **Why normalize by `tiles_moved`, not `move_path_cost` (revised 2026-07-22 re-review).** Dividing a pure advance by `move_path_cost` made `action_score ≈ POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED / move_cost`, so only `move_cost`-1 units (Scout) cleared `PASS_THRESHOLD` for a standing-start advance while Troopers/Snipers (`move_cost` 2 → 0.08) and Heavies (`move_cost` 3 → 0.053) scored *below* it and would **never advance from spawn** — the exact "plays too passively" failure mode this section exists to prevent, resurfacing for 3/4 of the roster. Normalizing by tiles instead makes a full-distance advance score `POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED` (≈ 0.16) for **every** unit regardless of `move_cost` (see worked example below), clearing the pass bar so the AI actually closes distance — while still sitting far below any positive economic (~1.77) or combat (~1.0+) play, so a march stays a genuine last resort, never a preferred one. This exception is confined to the two non-resourced move verbs (pure advance, retreat); every resourced verb (attack/produce/build/research and move+attack combos) still divides by AP cost per CR-3.
  - **Term 1 — close toward the contested front.** Distance is measured to the **nearest live enemy entity** (unit or structure). *(Simplified from an earlier "nearest forward entity" phrasing in the 2026-07-22 re-review: "forward" was never operationally defined to the doc's own precision standard and contradicted AC-20. Plain nearest-enemy is the deterministic, testable definition; the minor cost — the AI may occasionally step toward an isolated flanking Scout rather than the main mass — is acceptable for the VS's deliberately-simple heuristic and can be revisited post-VS if playtest shows it matters.)* Deliberately small per-tile so a single idle shuffle stays a last resort.
  - **Term 2 — the push-enabler (forward-looking, added 2026-07-22).** `sets_up_attack_next_turn(dest)` is a cheap one-step check: from `dest`, could this unit legally attack some enemy *next* turn (is an enemy within `soft_move_cap + attack_range` of `dest`)? If so, add `SETUP_ADVANCE_BONUS`. This is what lets the AI **mass and advance to threaten** — closing distance *because it enables a strike next turn* — rather than only ever transacting-then-freezing. It reuses the reachability/target machinery already computed this loop; it is not lookahead search, just a one-tile "will this position threaten next turn" test. Without this term the AI delivered the doc's own named "plays too passively" failure mode: it froze in place between transactions and never mounted a push.
  - **Wounded-unit exclusion (anti-oscillation, added 2026-07-22 re-review).** A unit at or below `RETREAT_HP_FRACTION` of max hp *and* inside an enemy's next-turn threat range generates **only** its retreat candidate (below), **not** an advance/`SETUP_ADVANCE_BONUS` candidate. This prevents a two-turn advance→retreat→re-advance oscillation: without it, a wounded unit that retreats to just outside threat range could be scored *back* toward the front by Term 1/Term 2 the next turn, re-entering danger and re-triggering retreat — a visibly broken flail. Healthy units, and wounded units already out of threat range, are governed by the ordinary positional scoring above.
  - **Worked example — standing-start advance clears for all units.** A Heavy (`move_cost` 3) at the opening, no enemy in contact, advances its full budget of 3 tiles straight toward the nearest enemy: `dist_before − dist_after = 3`, `tiles_moved = 3`. `positional_value = 0.16 × 3 + 0 = 0.48` (no next-turn attack set up yet at range). `action_score = 0.48 / 3 = 0.16 > PASS_THRESHOLD (0.15)` ✓. A Scout advancing 3 tiles (`move_cost` 1): `positional_value = 0.48`, `tiles_moved = 3`, `action_score = 0.16` — **identical**, confirming `move_cost` no longer gates advancing. (Under the old `/move_path_cost` denominator the Heavy scored `0.48 / 9 = 0.053` and stood still.)

- **If an owned unit is wounded and in danger — self-preservation (added 2026-07-22):** any owned unit at or below `RETREAT_HP_FRACTION` of its max hp *and* currently inside an enemy's next-turn threat range generates a **retreat** move candidate:

  `retreat_value(move) = RETREAT_VALUE_PER_TILE_FLED × max(0, threat_dist_after − threat_dist_before)`

  scored via `action_score = retreat_value / tiles_moved` (the same tiles-normalized, non-resourced-move exception as the pure advance above — **not** `/ move_path_cost`, so a heavier wounded unit isn't taxed out of fleeing, and so the mechanic can't be silently zeroed by `move_cost`), valuing distance *away* from the nearest enemy that could attack it next turn. It competes on the same pass-threshold gate, so a genuinely endangered unit pulling back can out-score a marginal economy play — giving the AI a baseline "don't let your wounded units sit in the open to die" competence without any search. This is deliberately *not* full threat modeling (it doesn't defend structures or plan multi-unit screens — see OQ-3); it is the minimum self-preservation floor the Player Fantasy requires so the AI never reads as "doesn't know it's dying." A healthy unit (above `RETREAT_HP_FRACTION`) generates no retreat candidate and is governed by the ordinary positional/combat scoring above; a wounded, still-threatened unit generates *only* this retreat candidate (the anti-oscillation exclusion noted under the positional model above).

- **If a unit could move to a tile that enables an attack** (a move+attack combo): the candidate is scored as `combat_value` computed from `legal_targets(unit, from_tile)` (Combat's hypothetical-tile query) at the combined AP cost `move_path_cost(unit, tile) + attack_cost`. This is the same evaluation Command & Action Interface's D-3 marker surfaces to the human player, so the AI is never "smarter" about move+attack combos than what the player's own preview already promises is possible.

- **Cancel Build has no AP cost to divide by** (it returns AP, it doesn't spend it) — so `action_score(cancel_build) = cancel_build_value` directly, where `cancel_build_value = CANCEL_REFUND_RATE × build_cost(structure)`. Because a completed structure is normally worth more than half its cost, this rarely clears `PASS_THRESHOLD` against a concrete positive play — it's a rare safety-valve action, not a proactive strategy. The VS AI does not model "this structure is about to be destroyed" threat-awareness; that's out of scope for the simple heuristic (see Open Questions).

- **If two or more candidates tie on `action_score`** — "tie" meaning their scores differ by less than `SCORE_TIE_EPSILON` (Tuning Knobs), not a fragile raw-float `==`; the common exact case is two simultaneous lethal attacks both floored to the identical `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` constant: resolved by a deterministic tiebreak — lowest `ap_cost` first (spend the cheaper equal-value action, leaving more AP for the next loop iteration), then lowest entity ID as a final stable tiebreak. No randomness is ever used (Pillar 2 — deterministic decisions). *(Emergent consequence of the secondary key: because entity IDs come from a monotonic counter (Unit System), an exact score+cost tie resolves to the **oldest** eligible entity first — deterministic and harmless (IDs are not player-visible or influenceable), documented so "why does the AI always act with unit #3 first" has a paper trail.)*

- **If `apply_action()` unexpectedly rejects the chosen candidate**: a defensive case, not a normal pathway — nothing else mutates game state between the AI's own `clone()` and its commit within one loop iteration, so a legal-at-evaluation candidate should always still be legal at commit time. If it is ever rejected anyway, the interface handles it exactly as CR-2/States describe: discard the stale clone, re-loop from a fresh one, no AP spent, no crash.

- **If the AI has zero legal actions at TURN_START** (all producers/units destroyed, no legal moves, no affordable structures): it ends its turn immediately via CR-6 — a legitimate losing position, not a stall or a bug.

- **If a produced unit or newly-built structure becomes part of the very next loop iteration's candidate pool**: no special-casing needed — CR-2's re-`clone()` after every commit means the fresh enumeration automatically includes anything just created.

- **If the AI's Research Lab is destroyed mid-research**: `research_value` is only ever computed for a *new* research start, never as a "defend this investment" term — the AI simply re-decides whether to start research again next time it evaluates, exactly as if for the first time. No special loss-handling logic required.

## Tuning Knobs

| Knob | Default | Safe range | What it affects / what breaks at extremes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HP_PER_AP` | 1.5 | 1.0–2.0 | Global hp-to-AP exchange rate anchoring `combat_value` and `research_value`'s Attack/Defense Tech term. Lower values bias the AI toward booming (undervalues combat); higher values bias it toward rushing (overvalues combat). 1.5 matches the roster's best hp/AP efficiency (Trooper, Scout). |
| `KILL_DENIAL_RATE` | 0.5 | 0.3–0.7 | Fraction of a kill victim's sunk AP credited as bonus `combat_value`. Too low undervalues finishing blows relative to trading; too high over-prioritizes low-value kills (e.g. chasing a fleeing Scout) over higher-ROI economy plays. |
| `ECONOMY_HORIZON` | 6 turns | 4–10 | Future turns of income counted toward `economy_value` and Economy Tech's `research_value`. Too short undervalues booming (combat-forward AI); too long over-weights it. (Permanent Attack/Defense buffs use `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` instead, not this.) **⚠ Cross-knob invariant:** raising this raises `economy_value`'s uncapped ceiling — re-verify `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` still exceeds `economy_ceiling_score` (formula in that row) or CR-7 breaks. |
| `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` | 10 turns | 6–15 | Horizon for permanent army-wide buffs (Attack/Defense Tech) — longer than `ECONOMY_HORIZON` because a permanent buff pays for the whole remaining game. Too short (≤7) re-breaks Defense Tech (pushes it back below `PASS_THRESHOLD`); too long over-weights teching vs. combat/economy. |
| `ECONOMY_DECAY` | 0.85 | 0.7–0.95 | Per-turn discount on projected future value. Lower (heavier discount) makes the AI present-biased/combat-forward; near 1.0 approaches an undiscounted sum that over-weights long-horizon economy/tech. **⚠ Cross-knob invariant:** raising this raises `economy_value`'s uncapped ceiling (at 0.95 the first-outpost score alone ≈2.52, already above `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS`'s 2.5 floor) — re-verify `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` exceeds `economy_ceiling_score` or CR-7 breaks. |
| `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN` | 2 | 1–4 | **Secondary** cadence guardrail (replacing the old valuation cap): max economy-outpost + research commits the AI makes in one turn. Bounds a single-turn runaway boom *without* understating each investment's honest value (see the decoupling note in Formulas). Lower = more tempo-forward; too high removes the brake. **Provenance / limitation (2026-07-22 re-review):** the default of 2 is a plausible starting value, **not** derived from the quantitative economy model that justified the board pin — flagged for the same empirical validation as `ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE`. Per the economy-designer re-review it does **not** meaningfully bind until ~turn 3–4 on the pinned board (early on, income itself caps the AI at ~2 outposts/turn anyway) and only delays a given outpost count ~1–2 turns; the *primary* dominant-strategy guard is the pinned board + tile scarcity + `CLOSEOUT_TARGET_TURNS`. Treat this knob as a back-up, and re-derive it against a turn-by-turn model before Production (OQ-5). |
| `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` | 3.5 | 2.5–5 | Fixed `action_score` floor for a finishing/immediately-lethal attack (CR-7). Must stay above `economy_ceiling_score = OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER1 × Σ_{t=1}^{ECONOMY_HORIZON} ECONOMY_DECAY^t / economy_outpost.build_cost` (the uncapped first-outpost ceiling) or a kill could be skipped for a higher economy score — defeating CR-7. **This is a cross-knob invariant, not a static one:** at defaults `economy_ceiling_score ≈ 1.77` (3.5 clears comfortably), but at `ECONOMY_HORIZON`=10 + `ECONOMY_DECAY`=0.95 it rises to ≈3.81, which the default 3.5 does **not** clear. If `ECONOMY_HORIZON` or `ECONOMY_DECAY` is raised toward its safe-range max, raise `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` in lockstep (its own 2.5–5 range may need extending). A first-playtest / spike task should confirm the shipped trio (`LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS`, `ECONOMY_HORIZON`, `ECONOMY_DECAY`) satisfies the invariant. |
| `PASS_THRESHOLD` | 0.15 | 0.05–0.3 | Minimum `action_score` to act on; below this the AI ends its turn (CR-2/CR-6). Co-tuned with `ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE` and `POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED` — raising it much above ~0.16 pushes Attack/Defense Tech and efficient repositioning back below the line; too low risks the AI taking clearly-bad trades just to avoid hoarding AP. |
| `ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE` | 1.5 | 0.5–2 | Converts Attack/Defense Tech's flat stat bonus into a per-turn AP-equivalent rate. Now a defensible mid-range value (not reverse-tuned to clear a threshold — `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` does that work honestly). Check against real playtest attack-frequency data before production (OQ-2); at the very low end the AI would research Attack/Defense Tech more rarely. |
| `REACHABILITY_MULTIPLIER` | {0.9, 1.0, 1.1} | fixed 3-band | Board-context adjustment to `production_value`. Widening the band would let positioning-at-production-time swing unit value more than the roster's own `produce_cost` balancing intends — kept narrow deliberately. The three bands are selected by **contact state** (defined operationally under `production_value`). |
| `POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED` | 0.16 | 0.10–0.3 | Positional value per tile of distance closed toward the contested front, for a non-attacking move (Edge Cases). Because the pure-advance score is **tiles-normalized** (not AP-cost-divided), a full-distance closing move scores exactly this value for **every** unit regardless of `move_cost`, clearing `PASS_THRESHOLD` (0.15) at the default 0.16 — so the whole roster advances from a standing start, not just Scouts. `SETUP_ADVANCE_BONUS` still lifts a *threatening* advance decisively higher. **Range floor raised 0.05→0.10** (2026-07-22 re-review): at 0.05 even a perfect full-distance advance scored 0.05, below the 0.15 pass bar, silently disabling repositioning; the floor must stay above `PASS_THRESHOLD` for the mechanic to function. Do not raise so high (>~0.3) that an idle shuffle out-competes economy/combat. |
| `SETUP_ADVANCE_BONUS` | 0.4 | 0.1–0.8 | Flat `positional_value` bonus when a non-attacking move lands on a tile that lets the unit attack an enemy *next* turn (Edge Cases, push-enabler term). This is what makes the AI advance to threaten rather than freeze between transactions. Too low → passive AI returns; too high → the AI over-commits units forward into bad trades. |
| `RETREAT_HP_FRACTION` | 0.30 | 0.15–0.5 | An owned unit at/below this fraction of max hp *and* inside enemy next-turn threat range generates a retreat candidate (Edge Cases, self-preservation). Higher → AI retreats more eagerly (can read as timid); lower → AI lets more wounded units die. |
| `RETREAT_VALUE_PER_TILE_FLED` | 0.20 | 0.10–0.4 | Positional value per tile a wounded, endangered unit puts between itself and the nearest threat. Also **tiles-normalized** (not AP-cost-divided), so a full-distance flee scores exactly this value for any unit — the default 0.20 clears `PASS_THRESHOLD` and sits slightly above `POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED` so escaping real danger out-scores a marginal economy play, but not so high the AI flees from trades it should take. **Range floor raised 0.05→0.10** (2026-07-22 re-review, same reason as `POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED`): below `PASS_THRESHOLD` the retreat mechanic is silently inert. |
| `HQ_SIEGE_VALUE` | 12 | 9–15 | The `ap_cost_opponent_paid_for` weight for the enemy **HQ** (which has no `build_cost`). Set at/above the most expensive structure (Production Outpost, 9) so a legitimate siege on the win-condition target never scores below chipping an ordinary structure. Spike-gated like other combat numbers. Too low → AI under-prioritizes the actual win condition; too high → AI tunnel-visions the HQ over better local plays. |
| `CANCEL_REFUND_RATE` | 0.5 | 0.3–0.6 | Fraction of a structure's `build_cost` credited as `cancel_build_value` (Edge Cases). **Owned by Base & Production** (this GDD consumes its value, matching that doc's `CANCEL_REFUND_RATE`); listed here only because `cancel_build_value` reads it. |
| `SCORE_TIE_EPSILON` | 1e-6 | 1e-8 – 1e-4 | Two `action_score` values are treated as tied (triggering the Edge-Cases tie-break) if they differ by less than this. Defined so tie-detection is a stable tolerance comparison, not a fragile raw-float `==` (see the determinism note under `action_score`). Applies uniformly regardless of which verb-formula produced each score. 1e-6 is safely ~6 orders of magnitude above the worst-case float accumulation error of the longest chained formula (a 10-term `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` geometric sum, error ~1e-12), **assuming GDScript `float` is 64-bit double** (true in Redot/Godot 4.x) — if that ever changed, revisit this margin. |

**Knob interactions:** `PASS_THRESHOLD`, `POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED`, and `RETREAT_VALUE_PER_TILE_FLED` sit near a shared boundary (all are marginal-move scores compared against the pass bar) — don't adjust one without checking the others. The earlier tri-knob knife-edge (`PASS_THRESHOLD` / `ATTACKS_LANDED_PER_TURN_ESTIMATE` / `POSITIONAL_VALUE_PER_TILE_CLOSED` all pinned to one boundary so a single dial solved three problems) is **relaxed**: research now clears the bar via `TECH_VALUE_HORIZON` with real margin, decoupling the tech-research question from the repositioning question. Separately, `ECONOMY_HORIZON`, `ECONOMY_DECAY`, and `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN` together govern boom behavior — the first two set how much the AI *values* economy, the third caps how fast it can *act* on that value. **Critical cross-dependency (2026-07-22 re-review): `ECONOMY_HORIZON` and `ECONOMY_DECAY` also set `economy_value`'s uncapped ceiling, which `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` must dominate for CR-7 to hold** — these three knobs are coupled, not independent. Raising either economy knob toward its safe-range max pushes the first-outpost `action_score` up (≈1.77 at defaults → ≈3.81 at both maxima), and at the high end it exceeds the default `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` (3.5), silently letting a non-lethal economy build outscore a finishing blow. Never raise `ECONOMY_HORIZON`/`ECONOMY_DECAY` without recomputing `economy_ceiling_score` (formula in the `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` rows) and confirming `LETHAL_FLOOR_BONUS` still exceeds it. On the dominant-strategy brake itself: the **primary** guard is the pinned 14×16 board + tile scarcity + `CLOSEOUT_TARGET_TURNS`; `MAX_ECONOMY_INVESTMENTS_PER_TURN` is a **secondary** cadence brake that (per the economy-designer re-review) does not bind until ~turn 3–4 on the pinned board and only delays a given outpost count by ~1–2 turns — do not over-credit it as the sole line of defense (see OQ-5 and the decoupling note in Formulas).

## Visual/Audio Requirements

**N/A — no direct presentation surface.** The AI Opponent is a decision-making backend; every effect of its choices (unit moves, attacks, builds, research starts) renders through the same visual/audio language already specified by Movement, Combat Resolution, Base & Production, and Game HUD's own Visual/Audio sections. There is no AI-specific overlay, indicator, or cue — the player should never be able to tell "this move came from the AI" by anything other than it being the opponent's turn (per CR-4's "no privileged information" principle, extended to presentation: the AI's actions must look exactly like a human player's actions would).

**One presentation-relevant constraint (perceived pacing).** Although the AI has no overlay of its own, each committed action should **render as it resolves** — streamed one commit at a time through the shared visual language — not computed as a silent batch and revealed at the end. This bounds *perceived* AI-turn latency to the per-commit gap rather than the whole-turn compute time (see OQ-1 and AC-9b), and makes the rival legible: the player watches it act move-by-move, exactly as a hot-seat opponent's turn reads. A brief inter-commit pacing delay (owned by Game HUD / presentation, not this system) may be desirable so fast turns don't flash by unreadably.

## UI Requirements

**N/A — no direct UI surface.** The AI Opponent has no menu, panel, or interactive element of its own; it consumes the same query interfaces the player's Command & Action Interface consumes, but never renders anything itself.

| OQ-1 | **Performance budget & enumeration cost for the evaluate→commit loop.** CR-2 re-`clone()`s and *fully re-enumerates* every owned entity's every verb after **each** commit — an O(K × N × W×H) shape (K commits, N units, W×H board); since K scales with N this is ~O(N² × W×H) worst-case. On the pinned 14×16 board with a mid/late-game army this is plausibly hundreds of ms to low seconds of GDScript per AI turn — a *perceptible* turn stall (not a frame-budget violation; turn-based). **Design-level commitments made now** (not blindly deferred): (a) the VS accepts full re-enumeration as a deliberate simplicity-over-speed choice and **pre-authorizes** `/create-architecture` to substitute an incremental-invalidation / dirty-set enumeration (only re-score entities whose legal-action set could have changed since the last commit) *without* that counting as a CR-2 redesign or a re-review — **but only if the ADR first proves the incremental strategy produces the identical candidate ranking and tie-break outcome as full re-enumeration** for any game state (e.g. via a differential test comparing both strategies across the AC-7 fixture corpus). This proof is required because scoring terms like `positional_value` depend on *other* units' positions, not just the scored unit's own legality, so a naive dirty-set that only re-touches entities whose own legality changed could silently reorder candidates and violate CR-2/CR-7/AC-3's determinism + tie-break guarantees. If behavioral equivalence cannot be established, the substitution needs a design-level sign-off, not a silent swap. (b) a stated worst-case army size **N ≤ 24** for the VS to size the budget against (no unit cap exists corpus-wide — this is the number architecture designs to; revisit if the roster grows; a playtest tripwire flagging observed armies > 24 would falsify the assumption rather than let it rot). (c) responsiveness is bounded by *per-commit* streaming, not per-turn batch (AC-9b + the Visual/Audio note). The concrete ms/call budget **and a caching strategy that does not yet exist anywhere in this corpus** remain the ADR's job: contrary to an earlier draft of this note, movement-system.md and combat-resolution.md do **not** hand this doc a `reachable()`/`legal_targets(from_tile)` memoization design — both only *defer a budget number* to this doc/the ADR, and movement-system.md carries an explicit **no-stale-cache invariant** that any future cache (across the many cloned states the AI evaluates) must be reconciled with. Note also the complexity constant is dominated by the move+attack combo scan (per-unit cost is `reachable`-frontier-sized, not O(1)) plus the new per-tile retreat / push-enabler checks — so the `O(N²·W·H)` headline understates the real per-iteration constant. | technical-director (ADR) + performance-analyst | During `/create-architecture` |

| OQ-7 | **Relative-economy blindness + "economy-first accountant" tendency.** The scoring model reads only the AI's own board — no term is conditioned on the opponent's `income`, `completed_outpost_count`, or tech progress, so the AI plays identically whether ahead or behind on economy. It cannot out-boom, counter-boom, or preferentially raid an exposed enemy Lab/Outpost. **Related, flagged independently by game-designer + economy-designer in the 2026-07-22 re-review:** under default tuning a first Economy Outpost (`action_score` ≈1.77) out-scores production (≈1.10) and a non-lethal attack (≈1.00), so on any non-forced turn with a build tile available the AI's single best move is the economy building — a "raced by an accountant" flavor of passivity distinct from the freezing one, and by-construction correct ROI rather than a formula defect. Track in first playtest as an explicit failure signature ("AI feels like an accountant / never contests the board early"); a cheap first step is a small multiplier on `combat_value` for enemy economic structures when `income(opponent) > income(self)`, and/or a slight down-weight of `economy_value` when behind on board presence. | economy-designer / game-designer | Post-VS, or first playtest if the AI reads as economy-oblivious / economy-first |
| OQ-8 | **One-ply baitability (ship-and-validate, per creative-director).** The greedy density model has no lookahead, so a human can dangle a cheap unit to lure an AI unit into a losing next-turn trade. Deliberately **not** fixed for the VS — the simple heuristic is the stated scope, and the new retreat term blunts the worst cases *for already-wounded units*. **Caveat (2026-07-22 re-review):** `SETUP_ADVANCE_BONUS` is itself a *new* source of baitable positions — it actively pulls a **healthy** unit toward tiles that threaten next turn, which is exactly the tile profile a bait works from, and retreat only triggers after the unit is already wounded (one turn too late to have prevented a bad trade). So the new terms blunt some cases and mildly widen others; net baitability remains an accepted VS risk. If playtest shows the exploit is discovered fast and collapses the "credible rival" feel, the scoped fix is a bounded 1-ply counter-check on the top few candidates (simulate the opponent's single best counter, discount a candidate that creates a clearly-losing exchange) — a heuristic filter, not full search. | game-designer | First playtest; implement the 1-ply filter only if the exploit proves corrosive |

| OQ-10 | **Wounded-unit oscillation is closed in-doc, but the exclusion is a blunt instrument.** The advance/retreat oscillation risk (a wounded unit fleeing to just outside threat range, then being scored back toward the front next turn) is resolved by the Edge-Cases wounded-unit exclusion (a wounded, threatened unit generates *only* a retreat candidate).
