## Overview

The **Game HUD** is the persistent, always-on information layer that surfaces the game's state so the player can read the board at a glance. Where the Command & Action Interface (#9) handles *acting* — selection, previews, and committing — the HUD handles *knowing*: it is a read-only aggregation of state from Game State & Turn Manager, AP & Credits Economy, and the Core gameplay systems, rendered as durable on-screen chrome that is never selected or clicked-through. It owns **two first-class budget counters** — the **AP counter** (the *tactical* budget for move/attack, shown as a first-class neon element with an on-demand AP-carryover indicator) and the **Credits counter** (the *banked economic* war chest, shown as a co-equal first-class neon element with an on-demand Credit income breakdown) — plus the **turn/round indicator** and **"YOUR TURN / ENEMY TURN"** banner, the **action log**, the **unit and structure info panels** (hp as chunky pips, attack, move cost, has-acted state, build timers, production capacity, research status and buffs), and the **victory/defeat presentation**. It computes nothing about balance — every value it shows is read from the system that owns it — so its single job is to make the authoritative game state *legible*. Without the HUD the player would be flying blind: unable to see how much AP or how many Credits they have, whose turn it is, how hurt a unit is, or whether they've won. With it, the entire depth of the tempo duel stays readable (Pillar 3), and both economies stay *felt* — the AP counter fills at the start of each turn and ticks down with every tactical spend, while the Credits counter grows each turn with income and ticks down on each economic spend, so the player always senses both the budget they are racing *and* the war chest they are banking. The two must read as visually distinct (different hue families) so the tactical tempo and the economic war chest never blur. The HUD and the Command & Action Interface are deliberately split along a *know vs. act* line, and this document restates that boundary at each seam so the two never duplicate or contradict each other.

## Player Fantasy

**The feeling: I always know exactly where I stand.**

A great HUD is felt in two registers at once. The **direct** register is the two budget counters — the twin pulses of the whole game. The **AP counter** is the *beating pulse* of tactical tempo: at the start of your turn it *fills* with a satisfying flourish to your flat budget plus whatever you carried over, and you watch it *tick down* with each spend, chunky and discrete, so you physically feel your tactical budget draining as you commit to a plan. Alongside it, the **Credits counter** is the co-equal readout of your *banked economic war chest* — it doesn't reset; it *accumulates*. Each turn it grows by your income, and you watch the pile build, deciding *when* to spend it: hold and let it stack toward a big investment, or cash it out now. Both are the numbers your eyes return to between every decision; between them live "one more turn," "I'm one spend from my power spike," and "two more turns of banking and I can afford the whole tech." The two counters must read as visually distinct (different hue families) so tempo (AP) and war chest (Credits) never blur into one another. The **ambient** register is everything else — whose turn it is, how hurt that Heavy is, how many turns until your outpost finishes, whether your opponent has teched up — information you absorb at a glance without ever consciously "checking." The honest reference point is an RTS player's read of a minimap + resource bar — a fast, practiced, peripheral scan of several semi-live readouts at once — not the static single-glance of a chess position (see Visual/Audio D, which sizes the real late-game legibility problem and mandates a playtest for it). The *aspiration* is chess-like effortlessness; the *thing being read* is denser and moving, and the design is budgeted as such.

**The anchor moment**: you glance up mid-turn. The AP counter reads **6** in bright neon; beside it the Credits counter reads **21** in its own distinct hue, and you know one more turn of banking clears the tech you want. The turn banner says it's yours. The enemy Trooper across the board shows two hp pips missing — one good hit from dead. Your outpost badge reads *1 turn left*. In a single sweep of the eyes you have your whole situation — tactical budget, war chest, threats, and timers — and you spend the 6 knowing exactly what it buys while deciding to *hold* the 21. You never stopped to *read a menu* — the state was simply *there*, legible, waiting. That frictionless awareness is the HUD doing its job.

This is the ambient half of **Pillar 3** — *"the state is always visually legible at a glance."* The Command & Action Interface makes your *choices* legible before you commit; the HUD makes your *situation* legible at all times. Its highest compliment, like #9's, is to disappear: the player should feel like a commander with total battlefield awareness, not someone operating a dashboard. The *persistent* elements allowed to draw the eye on purpose are the two budget counters — the AP counter and the Credits counter — because in a game about spending two budgets wisely, each budget deserves to be a thing you can't stop watching. They are held to distinct hue families so the two persistent draws never compete as the same read (tempo vs. war chest). (Two *transient* elements — the turn banner and the victory/defeat screen — are also full-saturation neon, but they are momentary "this matters" beats that self-clear, not standing competitors for the eye; see Visual/Audio A and D.)

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

**CR-1 — Pure read, reactive render.** The HUD reads authoritative state from the owning systems and **never mutates game state**. It re-renders in response to state-change events (turn change, AP spend, hp change, build/research tick, entity added/removed). It contains no game logic and computes no balance value — every number it shows is read from the system that owns it.

> **Render-event contract (owed to `/architecture-decision`, not resolvable in this GDD).** CR-1's "reactive render" is intentionally **push, not poll** — but the concrete signal contract is an architecture decision, flagged here with a candidate shape so the ADR has a seed rather than a blank slate:
> - **Single event surface, not five.** Rather than the HUD subscribing directly to five gameplay singletons (which would couple a "leaf" node to every system), the candidate pattern is a **signal-bus autoload** all owning systems emit through, with the HUD as one subscriber. The ADR picks: one aggregate `state_changed` signal with a typed diff payload, vs. a small set of typed signals (`ap_changed`, `credits_changed`, `hp_changed(entity_id, old, new)`, `entity_added/removed`, `turn_changed`, `build_tick`). Either is acceptable; the ADR must pick one and define payloads + **synchronous same-call-stack emission ordering** (so AC-14's "resolution order" for the log is deterministic, not race-dependent).
> - **Dirty-flag coalescing.** "Re-render" means *reflect the new value*, **not** issue one redraw per event. The HUD marks dirty on signal and redraws at most once per frame (Godot `_process`/`NOTIFICATION_DRAW`), so a single commit that fires N events (e.g. a multi-hit kill) produces one coalesced redraw, not N.
> - **Read-only facade for the never-mutates guarantee.** Godot has no compile-time access modifiers, so "never mutates" cannot be enforced by types alone. Candidate: the HUD is injected with a **read-only facade** (`GameStateReader`, getters only — no `apply_action`, no setters) rather than the live mutable state object, so a stray mutating call is *unreachable* from HUD scripts, not merely discouraged. The ADR should also define a lint rule restricting which autoload methods `src/ui/` scripts may call. This makes AC-2 structurally true instead of review-enforced.

**CR-2 — Persistent regions.** Throughout the active player's turn the HUD always shows: the **AP counter**, the **Credits counter**, the **turn/round indicator**, the **End Turn control** (placement only — interaction is #9's), the **Build button**, and the **action log**. These never disappear mid-turn.

**CR-3 — AP counter (first-class neon, *tactical* budget).** The counter is one of the game's two most prominent numbers (the Credits counter, CR-3d, is its co-equal). It **fills with a flourish** to `FLAT_AP_PER_TURN + carry` at start-of-turn — the flat tactical grant plus whatever unspent AP carried over (capped at `AP_CARRYOVER_CAP`) — and shows a **discrete, chunky tick-down on each real commit** — and, per #9's locked rule, it **animates only on a real commit**, never on hover, cancel, illegal click, or selection. During a #9 preview it shows the projected result **inline** as `current → projected` (e.g. `9 → 3`), where the projected value is #9's `projected_remaining_ap`; the counter's *committed* value changes only when an action actually commits. Because AP is now a **flat, non-income-driven** budget, there is no AP income to decompose; instead an **on-demand AP-carryover indicator** shows how much was carried into this turn — `+N carried, capped at 5` (where N is the AP that survived the last end-of-turn, capped at `AP_CARRYOVER_CAP`) — so the player understands *why* the counter started where it did. (The old economic income breakdown now belongs to the Credits counter, CR-3d.) AP **carries** rather than discards at end of turn: any unspent AP up to the cap survives into the next fill, so the counter is never "wiped" — leftover simply rolls into the next start-of-turn value.

**CR-3a — AP counter animation states (explicit, so the counter can't get mushy).** The counter is a small state machine, not free-form animation. Exactly one *committed value* exists at a time; the states govern how that value and the preview echo present. **Whose AP the state machine is running over is an orthogonal dimension** (see CR-3b): the states below apply to whichever player's `current_ap` the counter is currently showing — the *local* player's during their own turn (full first-class neon, all four states reachable), or the *opponent's* during theirs (muted/`OPPONENT`-labelled, and only the **Committed (rest)** and — if `SHOW_OPPONENT_FILL_FLOURISH` is on — **Fill flourish** states are reachable; **Preview echo** is never reachable over opponent AP because only the local player's #9 can drive a preview, and **Tick-down** plays for the opponent's own commits during their turn). The whose-AP identity only changes at a turn boundary, never mid-turn.

| State | Trigger | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Committed (rest)** | default | Static number, no motion. This is the "trust" state — no motion means the value is real. |
| **Fill flourish** | start-of-turn AP reset (`PlayerTurn` entry) | Committed value animates up to `FLAT_AP_PER_TURN` + carryover over `AP_FILL_FLOURISH_MS`, once. |
| **Tick-down** | a real `apply_action` commit spending N AP | Committed value steps down by exactly N over `AP_TICK_DURATION_MS`, discrete/chunky. |
| **Preview echo** | #9 has an active preview | Committed value stays frozen; a `→ projected` echo renders beside it (see snap rule below). |

> **Transition rules (resolve the conflicts the panel flagged):**
> - **Fill flourish and preview echo are mutually exclusive by construction — *over the same player's AP*.** A local-player preview requires a prior select+verb inside the local Action phase, which cannot coexist with that player's pre-action turn-start fill. Across the whose-AP boundary the two also cannot stack, because a preview echo is only ever rendered over *local* AP while a fill flourish that could collide is either the local player's own (same-player case, above) or the opponent's — and the opponent's fill runs only after the whose-AP identity has already flipped and the local preview has been force-cleared (next rule). The exclusivity is therefore construction-guaranteed in every case, not merely the same-player one.
> - **On any `PlayerTurn`/`EndTurn` transition the preview echo is force-cleared *synchronously*, before the incoming turn's fill-flourish trigger is allowed to evaluate.** This closes the one-frame race: #9's FSM exit-to-`IDLE` and the HUD's turn-start fill are driven by different signals with no inherent ordering guarantee, so the HUD must not *infer* the echo is gone — it explicitly tears the echo down as the first step of handling the turn transition, then evaluates the fill. A stale echo can never be visible in the same frame as a fill flourish. (This also force-clears any echo left open by a mouse held over a now-invalid tile when the turn ends.)
> - **A commit that also triggers `GameOver` forces any in-flight AP tick-down to complete instantly** (snap the committed value to its final post-spend number within the same one-frame bound AC-17 requires for the victory/defeat overlay). The AP counter must not be left frozen at a mid-tick intermediate value under the overlay. The hp-pip drain from that same killing commit is the exception — it is **not** truncated (the destruction that *causes* the win is Player-Fantasy load-bearing and should read fully), but it is likewise not *gated*: it plays out beneath/alongside the overlay without delaying the overlay's one-frame appearance. `GameOver` is thus a terminal transition out of every CR-3a state, resolving the counter to rest.
> - **The preview echo SNAPS — no tween** on open, on value-change (re-hovering a different tile/target), and on close/cancel. It inherits #9's hover-preview rule ("numbers snap in, never count up") because it is displaying #9's number. Rationale: a fast mouse-sweep across several reachable tiles must not produce a flickering fade next to the "only-moves-on-commit" committed value — motion is reserved for real commits (CR-3).
> - **Rapid sequential commits are serialized upstream, not queued here.** #9's `INPUT_LOCK_MS ≥ AP_TICK_DURATION_MS` guarantees one tick fully resolves before the next commit dispatches (see Dependencies → #9). The HUD therefore **must not** build its own tick queue/interrupt logic; it renders one tick at a time as commits arrive. (If any non-#9 caller — AI turn, future replay — could commit outside that input lock, the tick simply plays for each arriving commit; it never overlaps a prior tick mid-flight because the committed value is single-valued.)
> - **hp-pip drains and an AP tick from the same resolution animate concurrently** (different elements, no shared serialization); only same-element (AP counter) commits serialize.

**CR-3b — Whose counters are shown (perfect-information disambiguation, both budgets).** In a no-fog game **both** counters show the **active player's** values at all times — `current_ap` on the AP counter and `current_credits` on the Credits counter — so during the opponent's turn they display *their* budgets. Because the counters are habitual, low-attention glance targets (CR-3, CR-3d), swapping whose numbers they are every turn boundary is a consistent-mapping hazard. Resolution: whenever *either* counter shows a *non-local* player's value, it carries a **persistent `OPPONENT` label and a muted/desaturated treatment** for the entire opponent turn (not merely the transient turn banner, which self-clears after `TURN_BANNER_DURATION_MS`). The local player's own AP and Credits are the only values shown in full first-class neon. `SHOW_OPPONENT_AP` (default on) gates whether the opponent's *both* budgets appear at all; this rule governs how they're marked when they do. This makes "I always know exactly where I stand" hold even while watching the opponent — the surveillance read of *both* their budgets is explicitly framed as *theirs*, never mistakable for *yours*.

**CR-3d — Credits counter (first-class neon, *banked economic* war chest).** The Credits counter is the AP counter's co-equal — the second persistent budget the eye is allowed to rest on — but it obeys **accumulate-don't-reset** semantics, mirroring the AP counter's animation states where they make sense with one key difference: its start-of-turn fill *adds to* the running total rather than overwriting it. It shows `current_credits`, a banked pool that grows each turn and never discards. Its states:
- **Committed (rest):** static number, no motion — the "trust" state, same as the AP counter's.
- **Income flourish (add-to-running-total):** at start-of-turn the counter animates a `+income` addition *onto* the existing pile — it does **not** re-fill from zero or overwrite; the value climbs from `current_credits` to `current_credits + credit_income(player)`. This is the visible "your war chest grew" beat.
- **Tick-down:** on each economic (Credit) commit, a discrete/chunky tick-down by exactly the Credit cost spent, over `AP_TICK_DURATION_MS` (reused feel), identical in character to the AP tick so a spend reads the same on both counters.
- **Preview echo:** during a #9 preview of an economic action, the counter shows the projected result inline as `current → projected` (its share of the dual-cost preview; see CR-8) — its committed value moves only on a real commit, same rule as AP.

An **on-demand Credit income breakdown** reveals *why* income is what it is — `base` + tiered-outpost bonus + econ-tech bonus, each a **displayed field** from `Credits.credit_income_breakdown(player)`'s `{base, outpost, econ_tech}` return (never a HUD-side sum). The Credits counter **never discards** — Credits bank indefinitely, so unlike the AP counter it has no notion of "wiped at end of turn"; the pile simply persists across turns and grows. It is held to a **distinct hue family** from the AP counter (CR-3, Player Fantasy) so tempo and war chest never blur.

**CR-4 — Turn/round indicator & banners.** The HUD displays whose turn it is (`active_player`) and the round (`round_number`, shown as "Round N"), and raises a **"YOUR TURN / ENEMY TURN" banner** on each `PlayerTurn` transition so the player is never unsure whose turn it is (Pillar 3).

**CR-5 — On-board readouts (HUD-owned, always visible, not selection-tied).** Drawn on the board over the relevant entity, independent of what is selected: unit **hp as chunky pips** + a **has-acted/spent** marker + a per-unit **tech marker** (if the owner has researched); structure **hp**, a **turns-remaining badge** on under-construction structures, and a **research-in-progress marker** on a working Research Lab.

**CR-6 — Detail info panel (seam with #9).** A panel shows the **full stats** of the entity #9 currently has selected or is inspecting (type, current/max hp, effective attack, move cost, has-acted state; for structures: hp, build/production/research status). **#9 owns *which* entity and *what* data** (it owns selection/inspection); the **HUD owns the panel's presence, layout, and style.** The panel is empty when nothing is selected.

- **Persistent selection vs. transient inspection must read differently.** #9 draws a hard line between *selecting* your own entity (persistent, can lead to a command) and *inspecting* any entity on hover (transient, read-only, reverts on mouse-out). Because the panel content silently swaps between these as the cursor rolls over the board, the panel MUST carry a **distinct visual state** for the two — e.g. a "pinned/selected" treatment (solid accent edge, persistent) vs. a "peek/inspecting" treatment (dashed or dimmed edge, clearly temporary) — so the player never loses track of what their *actual* selection is while comparing enemy stats. Exact treatment defers to `/ux-design` + `/art-bible`; the *requirement that the two be distinguishable by more than content alone* is binding here (see Accessibility E). This also protects against the faction-hue-accent being the only differentiator (the recurring colorblind gap, OQ-3).
- **Data-flow direction (protects the "HUD is a leaf" claim).** The HUD **owns** the panel `Control` scene and its theme, and learns *what to show* by **subscribing outward-in to #9's `selection/inspection changed` signal** — #9 must NOT call into an HUD-owned node's API. If #9 reached into the panel directly, #9 would depend on the HUD and the "Downstream dependents: None" claim (Dependencies) would be false. This direction is an owed input to `/architecture-decision`.

**CR-7 — Action log (concise).** One line per committed action, newest on top, scrollable, retaining the last `ACTION_LOG_LENGTH` entries. Each entry is an **icon + short text** (e.g. "P1 Scout moved", "P2 built Production Outpost", "Trooper destroyed", "P1 Attack Tech complete"). One committed `apply_action` = one entry.

**CR-8 — Build button + dual-cost affordability (seam with #9).** A **persistent, affordability-aware Build button** (plus a hotkey) is the player-level entry to building structures. Pressing it opens structure-type selection; from there the flow is **handed to #9's build-placement preview and atomic commit** — the HUD owns the *button*, #9 owns the *preview and commit*. Because economic actions are now **dual-cost** (Credits + a small AP surcharge, both-or-neither), affordability reads `credits_can_afford AND ap_can_afford`: the button is dimmed when no structure type is affordable **on both pools**, and any economic-action preview shows **both costs** (e.g. `9 ⛁ + 2 AP`) and greys out if **either** pool falls short — with the **binding pool made clear** (which of the two is the one you can't cover), so the player knows whether they need to bank more Credits or free up AP. **Handoff-active cue:** while #9's `PREVIEW_BUILD` state is live, the Build button holds a visible **pressed/active** treatment (driven by subscribing to #9's build-preview state), so a player who opened Build then cancels (#9's Cancel gesture) has HUD-side confirmation they are back to rest — the button they pressed is legibly the thing that was driving the overlay.

**CR-9 — Victory/defeat presentation.** On `match_status = GameOver(winner)`, the HUD presents a victory or defeat screen naming the winner, driven by `win_condition` (opponent HQ destroyed). If `MAX_ROUNDS` is enabled (off in VS) and reached, the presentation reflects the `TIEBREAK_METRIC` outcome.

**CR-10 — Turn-scoped interactivity.** The HUD's two interactive controls (End Turn placement, Build button) are live only during the active player's Action phase. During the opponent's turn and resolution phases the HUD still **displays** all state (read-only) but its controls are inert — consistent with #9's inspection-only rule (CR-11 there).

**CR-11 — No duplication of #9.** The HUD does **not** perform selection, previews/overlays, the action menu, or commit routing — those are #9's. The HUD is persistent information display plus the AP counter, log, banners, on-board readouts, and the two hosted controls (whose *interaction* routes to #9 / the turn manager).

### States and Transitions

The HUD is reactive; its display mode follows `match_status`:

| `match_status` | HUD display mode |
|---|---|
| `Setup` | Pre-match / minimal chrome; no AP counter activity yet |
| `PlayerTurn(P)` where P = local player | **Full interactive HUD**: AP + Credits counters active, Build + End Turn live, "YOUR TURN" banner on entry, AP filled to flat+carry and Credits grown by income |
| `PlayerTurn(P)` where P = opponent | **Display-only**: all readouts live, controls inert, "ENEMY TURN" banner on entry. The AP **and** Credits counters show the opponent's `current_ap` / `current_credits` under a **persistent `OPPONENT` label + muted/desaturated treatment** (see CR-3b) — not just the transient banner — so whose budgets are on screen is never ambiguous |
| `EndTurn(P)` | Transient — unspent AP **carried** (up to `AP_CARRYOVER_CAP`) into the next reset and Credits **banked** (never discarded), so neither counter is wiped; hands to next `PlayerTurn`. **Both controls (Build, End Turn) are inert in this state** (the turn has already been handed off); no HUD input is accepted until the next `PlayerTurn(local)` — this closes the gap where CR-10's "Action phase" rule would otherwise leave `EndTurn(P)` control-liveness undefined |
| `GameOver(winner)` | **Victory/defeat overlay** naming the winner; HUD input otherwise frozen |

Transient overlays layered on top: the **turn banner** (on each `PlayerTurn` transition), the **Credit income breakdown** and **AP-carryover indicator** (on-demand toggles), and the **detail info panel** (follows #9's current selection/inspection).

### Interactions with Other Systems

## Formulas

**The Game HUD owns no formulas.** Like the Command & Action Interface, it is bound by a **pass-through discipline**: every value it displays is read from the system that owns it, and the HUD holds **zero balance constants**. There is no math to define here — only trivial 1:1 display mappings, listed for completeness:

| Display mapping | Rule | Source of truth |
|---|---|---|
| **hp pips** | one pip per hp point; pips drain discretely as hp decreases | Unit / Base & Production hp (read) |
| **Credit income breakdown** | shows `Credits.credit_income_breakdown`'s own decomposition: `base` (`BASE_INCOME`, 10) + tiered-outpost `outpost` contribution + `econ_tech` (Economy-Tech) contribution — **displayed, not recomputed** | AP & Credits Economy `Credits.credit_income_breakdown` |
| **AP-carryover indicator** | shows the AP that carried into this turn as `+N carried, capped at 5` — read verbatim (N is the leftover survived at end-of-turn, `min(leftover, AP_CARRYOVER_CAP)`); **displayed, not recomputed** | AP & Credits Economy / Game State (`current_ap` reset inputs) |
| **Build-timer badge** | shows the owning system's `turns-remaining` verbatim | Base & Production build-timer |
| **Projected AP (`current → projected`)** | displays #9's `projected_remaining_ap` verbatim; the counter's committed value updates only on a real commit | Command & Action Interface `projected_remaining_ap` (D-1 there) |
| **Research progress** | shows the owning system's tech status/progress verbatim | Research / Tech |

### Pass-Through Invariant (mirrors #9)

> Every value the HUD displays MUST be the literal return of the owning system's read query, evaluated at render time. **The HUD holds zero copies of any owning-system balance constant** (`BASE_INCOME`, `credit_income` coefficients, `FLAT_AP_PER_TURN`, `AP_CARRYOVER_CAP`, hp values, costs, etc.). Any HUD code path that references a balance constant by name, or recomputes a value another system owns, is a violation. Like #9's, this is a structural constraint — enforce via code review / lint and a candidate implementation ADR, not a stated rule alone.
>
> **Explicit carve-out — local animation state is exempt.** Holding the *previous* displayed value purely to compute a transition delta for animation (the AP counter's tick-down needs `N = old − new` to step down "by exactly N"; the hp-pip drain needs `old_hp − new_hp` to know how many pips to animate off) is **not** a Pass-Through violation. This is transient presentation state, never an owned balance value, and it never overrides or substitutes for the owning system's current authoritative value — it only governs *how the display transitions to* that value. AC-21's stub-and-verify checks the *displayed final value* against the injected read query; an implementation that additionally retains one frame of prior state solely to tween toward that value still passes.
>
> **Required query-shape contract (feeds the ADR).** For the Credit income breakdown specifically, AP & Credits Economy must expose the decomposition as a *pre-labeled, pre-computed* return value — `Credits.credit_income_breakdown(player) → { base: int, outpost: int, econ_tech: int }` — so the HUD renders named fields it never assembles itself. The HUD must NOT receive the raw inputs (outpost count, tech flag) and compute the split locally; that path is exactly the invariant violation above. The same discipline applies to the AP-carryover indicator (the carried-over N is read, not recomputed from a stored end-of-turn leftover the HUD keeps) and to every row in the Formulas table: the owning system returns the display-ready value, the HUD only lays it out. Naming this contract is an owed input to `/architecture-decision` (see also the read-facade note under CR-1).

## Edge Cases

- **If nothing is selected**: the detail info panel is **empty/hidden**, not an error. On-board readouts (hp pips, badges) remain visible regardless of selection.
- **If it is the opponent's turn** (perfect-information game, no fog): the AP **and** Credits counters show the **active player's** `current_ap` / `current_credits` (read-only) under the persistent `OPPONENT` label + muted treatment (CR-3b), and all readouts stay live — the player can watch both of the opponent's budgets without mistaking either for their own. The HUD's controls are inert (CR-10); no fill/income flourish plays for the opponent unless configured.
- **If an entity's `max_hp` is at or above `PIP_MAX_HP`** (`max_hp ≥ PIP_MAX_HP`; e.g. HQ hp 40, Production Outpost 14 at the default 10): render hp as a **numeric `current/max` readout** instead of pips — pips are for low-hp units (`max_hp < PIP_MAX_HP`, default 10) where discrete pips read cleanly; anything at/above the threshold would produce an unreadable pip wall. The `≥` (not `>`) matters: at `PIP_MAX_HP = 14` a Production Outpost (hp exactly 14) must still flip to numeric, not render as 14 pips — the exact wall this rule exists to prevent. The chunky-discrete *feel* (Combat's requirement) is preserved by the number stepping down in whole integers, never a smooth bar.
- **If the Credit income breakdown is shown with 0 completed Economy Outposts**: it displays just `base` (`BASE_INCOME`, 10) — the `outpost` and `econ_tech` contribution lines are 0 and shown as such or omitted (never a phantom "+0 from tech" that implies a bonus exists).
- **If Economy Tech is held but 0 Economy Outposts exist**: the `econ_tech` contribution reads as **0** (it scales with completed Economy Outposts, of which there are none) — the breakdown must not imply the tech is currently earning anything. *Per the Pass-Through Invariant, this GDD deliberately names no Economy-Tech coefficient or outpost cap of its own — the `0` here is whatever `Credits.credit_income_breakdown`'s query returns for the `econ_tech` field; the coefficient (`ECONOMY_TECH_INCOME_BONUS`) and cap (`ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD`) live in AP & Credits Economy and must never be restated as literals in HUD prose or code.*
- **If the AP-carryover indicator is shown with 0 carried AP** (a full spend last turn, or the very first turn): it reads `+0 carried` or is omitted — never a phantom carry. Carry is capped at `AP_CARRYOVER_CAP` (5), so it never shows `+6`+; the displayed N is the owning system's `min(leftover, AP_CARRYOVER_CAP)`, read verbatim.
- **If an economic action's dual-cost preview is unaffordable on Credits but affordable on AP (or vice-versa)**: the preview greys out and names the **binding pool** (the one short) — e.g. Credits-short shows the Credit cost in a deny treatment while the AP surcharge reads affordable — so the player knows which pool to fix. Affordability is `credits_can_afford AND ap_can_afford`; a shortfall on *either* greys the action, never a partial/half-affordable state.
- **If #9 has an active preview of an *unaffordable/disabled* action**: the inline AP display shows the **current value only** with an "insufficient AP" indicator (mirroring #9's rule) — never `9 → -1` or a negative projected number.
- **If #9 has no active preview**: the AP counter shows the plain committed value with no `→` arrow.
- **If the action log exceeds `ACTION_LOG_LENGTH`**: the oldest entries scroll off / are dropped; the newest are always retained and visible.
- **If multiple events fire from one committed action** (e.g. an attack that kills a unit *and* destroys the HQ = win): the log records them in resolution order, and the **victory/defeat overlay takes precedence** over any turn banner or other transient.
- **If a turn banner and a `GameOver` transition would show at once**: the **victory/defeat presentation wins**; no "YOUR TURN" banner shows on the final transition. **This includes a banner already mid-flight**: `GameOver` immediately truncates/skips any in-progress *or* pending banner — the victory/defeat overlay must appear **within one frame** of the `GameOver` transition, never after the banner's remaining `TURN_BANNER_DURATION_MS` (up to 2000 ms) elapses. The win must read as immediate (Player Fantasy), not gated behind a stale banner's leftover hold. The turn-change stinger is likewise ducked/preempted by the victory-defeat cue (Audio precedence).
- **If no structure type is currently affordable**: the **Build button stays visible but dimmed** (so the affordance is still discoverable); activating it opens the picker with all types disabled (handed to #9's affordability gating), never hiding Build entirely.
- **If multiple Research Labs are working in parallel**: each shows its **own** research-in-progress marker (techs parallelize per Research/Tech).
- **If a unit carries both a has-acted marker and a tech marker** (and low hp pips): the three readouts must be laid out so none **occludes** the hp pips — hp legibility takes priority (it is the most time-critical readout).
- **If the entity whose detail panel is open is destroyed**: the panel **clears** (it follows #9's selection, which deselects a removed entity).

## Tuning Knobs

> **All knobs are UX-feel values.** Per the Pass-Through Invariant the HUD owns no balance constant; these affect legibility and feel only.

| Knob | Default | Safe range | What it affects / breaks at extremes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PIP_MAX_HP` | 10 | 6–14 | hp **below** → chunky pips; **at or above** (`≥`) → numeric `current/max` readout. Too high = unreadable pip walls on structures (HQ 40); too low = units lose the satisfying discrete-pip feel. Bound so the highest-hp *unit* (Heavy, hp 10) still shows pips: keep `PIP_MAX_HP > 10` if Heavy should stay pips, or `= 10` to flip Heavy to numeric too |
| `ACTION_LOG_LENGTH` | 20 | 8–50 | Entries retained in the action log. Too low loses the useful trace of the turn; too high clutters and competes with the board |
| `AP_FILL_FLOURISH_MS` | 400 ms | 150–800 ms | Duration of the start-of-turn AP fill. Too short loses the "abundance" beat; too long delays the player getting into their turn |
| `AP_TICK_DURATION_MS` | 120 ms | 60–250 ms | Per-spend tick-down animation. Must stay discrete/chunky (Combat + #9 language); too long and rapid multi-spends feel laggy; too short and the spend isn't *felt* |
| `TURN_BANNER_DURATION_MS` | 1000 ms | 500–2000 ms | How long the YOUR/ENEMY TURN banner holds. Too short = missed; too long = interrupts flow every turn |
| `HUD_AUDIO_DUCK_MS` | 150 ms | 80–300 ms | Duck/suppress window for the HUD's own cues when several cluster (turn-transition stinger vs. AP-fill; simultaneous completions; victory/defeat vs. stinger). Too short = cues flange/stack; too long = a real second event feels swallowed |
| `SHOW_OPPONENT_AP` | on | on / off | Whether the AP **and** Credits counters show the opponent's `current_ap` / `current_credits` during their turn (perfect-information game). off hides both (a soft partial-info choice); on is the default and matches the no-fog identity. **⚠ Not purely UX-feel — mechanics-adjacent:** unlike the other knobs in this table, flipping this changes *information available for play*, not just legibility. With it on, a player can read the opponent's remaining AP (exact threat ranges for end-of-turn bluffing — "they have 2 AP left, my exposed Sniper is safe") *and* their banked Credit war chest (are they saving toward a spike?); off removes both reads entirely. It stays here because the HUD owns the *display*, but a balance/design owner should treat this as a knob that measurably moves the skill ceiling, not a cosmetic default |
| `SHOW_OPPONENT_FILL_FLOURISH` | off | on / off | Whether the AP fill flourish **and** the Credits income flourish play at the *opponent's* start-of-turn. off keeps both flourishes as a "your turn" signal; on shows both of the opponent's economies animating |
| `INCOME_BREAKDOWN_DEFAULT` | collapsed | collapsed / expanded | Whether the **Credit income breakdown** (and the AP-carryover indicator) start revealed or on-demand. expanded teaches new players *why* income/carry are what they are; collapsed keeps the HUD lean |

**Knob interactions:** `PIP_MAX_HP` interacts with unit/structure hp values owned elsewhere. The design intent is **all structures render numeric, all units render pips**. Structure hp values (per `base-production.md`/registry): Economy Outpost 8, Defensive Structure 10, Research Lab 12, Production Outpost 14, HQ 40 — the *lowest* is the Economy Outpost at **8**. Unit hp values: Scout 3, Trooper 6, Heavy 10, Sniper 3 — the *highest* is Heavy at **10**. To keep every unit as pips and every structure numeric, the threshold must sit **above the highest unit hp (10) and at/below the lowest structure hp (8)** — which is impossible to satisfy for the Economy Outpost (hp 8 < Heavy hp 10), so the Economy Outpost (hp 8; 8 < default `PIP_MAX_HP` of 10) correctly renders as **pips** at the default, not numeric. This is acceptable: an 8-pip structure is still readable; only the true walls (Production Outpost 14, HQ 40) must go numeric, and both do at any value in the safe range under the `≥` rule. **Second boundary case worth naming (the Defensive Structure at hp 10):** it sits *exactly* on the default threshold, so at `PIP_MAX_HP = 10` it renders numeric (`≥`), but at `PIP_MAX_HP = 11` — a legal safe-range value one might pick specifically to keep Heavy (hp 10) in pips — the Defensive Structure (hp 10) *also* flips to pips, joining the Economy Outpost as a second pip-rendered structure and further loosening the "all structures numeric" intent. This is the same trade-off, made explicit: raising the threshold to protect Heavy's pip feel necessarily pip-renders any structure at or below the new threshold. Neither outcome is a bug (a 10-pip structure is still readable), but the two knobs — "keep Heavy in pips" and "keep all structures numeric" — are in genuine tension and cannot both be fully satisfied with the current hp values; the default of 10 resolves it by prioritizing "structures numeric" (Heavy at exactly 10 flips to numeric too). `AP_FILL_FLOURISH_MS` + `AP_TICK_DURATION_MS` together set the AP counter's whole "feel" — tune as a pair so the fill feels generous and each tick feels sharp; as a floor, keep the fill meaningfully longer than a single tick (a fill shorter than one tick inverts the intended "generous fill / sharp spend" hierarchy).

### B · Layout / information-architecture principles (not pixels — deferred to `/ux-design`)

- **AP counter + Credits counter + turn/round indicator** share the **top-center spine** (primary sightline; "my tactical budget" + "my war chest" + "whose turn" in one glance) — peripheral-persistent, never central. The two counters sit adjacent but in distinct hue families so a single glance separates tempo from war chest.
- **Build + End Turn** anchor to a **fixed corner** (mirrors #9's fixed End Turn corner); **action log** to the **opposite edge**, collapsible.
- **Detail panel is edge-docked, not board-floating** — distinct from #9's on-board floating menu, so the two never collide (one on-board, one off-board).
- **On-board readouts** get **fixed per-tile sub-positions** (pips top edge, has-acted one corner, tech marker the other) so all coexist without stacking; **hp legibility wins** any conflict (per Edge Cases).
- **Board center stays clear** (Pillar 3 flagship): no panel/log/banner ever renders over the grid interior; only minimal per-tile readouts live inside board space. The **turn banner is the one deliberate transient exception** and self-clears before input is expected.

### C · Audio (synthwave-crisp; shared events fire once, never double-triggered)

> **Ownership discipline (each sound has exactly ONE `play()` call site).** "Shared" never means "two systems both fire it" — that is the double-trigger bug AC-20 exists to catch. A shared *asset* or shared *trigger signal* still has a single owning caller; every other interested system subscribes to the same signal for its *visual* reaction only and stays audio-silent. This mirrors how #9 resolves its own attack cue (Combat owns `play()`, #9 abstains). The precise audio-event-bus owner is an `/architecture-decision` item; the *single-owner rule* is binding here.

| Event | Character | Single audio owner (who calls `play()`) |
|---|---|---|
| AP fill (start of turn) | Ascending arpeggio (flat-budget beat, does not scale — AP is flat) | **Game State & Turn Manager's start-of-turn signal → one audio subscriber.** HUD animates the counter fill off the same signal but does **not** independently trigger the sound. (Not #9 — #9 has no turn-start hook.) |
| Credit income (start of turn) | A distinct cue from the AP fill, scaling with `credit_income` (the "war chest grew" beat) — timbrally separable from the AP-fill arpeggio so the two start-of-turn additions aren't misread as one | **The same Game State & Turn Manager start-of-turn signal → one audio subscriber.** Fires alongside the AP fill in the start-of-turn cluster (see mix-priority below); the HUD animates the Credits income flourish off the same signal but does **not** independently trigger the sound. |
| AP tick (per spend) | Sharp dry percussive tick, countable | **The single `apply_action`-committed signal → one audio subscriber.** #9's commit-flash and the HUD's counter tick both subscribe to that same signal for their *visuals*; neither plays the sound — one dedicated audio owner does. Audio must bind to the *commit* signal, never to an `ap_changed` value-change (which could fire for non-commit reasons and double-trigger). |
| Credit tick (per economic spend) | A tick in the same percussive family as the AP tick but timbrally distinct (so a Credit spend sounds like a Credit spend), countable | **The same single committed-action signal for economic actions → one audio subscriber.** A dual-cost commit spends both pools atomically, so the HUD may play the AP tick and Credit tick concurrently (different elements, no shared serialization); each still has exactly one owning `play()` call, never double-triggered off a value-change. |
| Turn-change stinger | Directional: brighter/ascending (your turn) vs. lower/descending (enemy) | HUD (new). **Supplementary** accessibility channel only (see E) — not load-bearing for whose-turn, since players mute SFX in long sessions. |
| Victory cue | Full synth fanfare, resolves upward | HUD (new) |

| Structure-complete | **Reuses #9's Build-stamp *asset*** ("investment lands" feeling, no new sound) — but fires when **Base & Production's build-timer hits 0**, potentially many turns after the build commit | **Base & Production (#7)** owns the trigger (its timer completes it). NOT #9 — #9's Build stamp fires on build-*commit*, a different moment with no hook into a later timer completion. Asset reuse ≠ trigger ownership. |

**Simultaneous / late-game completion stacking (parallels the multi-Research-Lab visual edge case).** When 2+ completion cues (Research-complete, Structure-complete) land in the same or adjacent frames — plausible mid-late game with several Labs + build queues — the audio owner **ducks/suppresses the 2nd+ simultaneous completion to a single played cue that frame** (the on-board markers still update individually); it never layers N identical chimes into a flanged stack. See AC-23.

**Audio mix-priority & GameOver precedence (the visual precedence rule needs an audio twin).** The start-of-turn boundary is a genuine multi-cue cluster, not just a pair: **Game State & Turn Manager's canonical start-of-turn sequence advances build-timers and research-timers (its step 3) immediately before it resets AP (flat+carry) and adds Credit income (step 4)** — see `base-production.md` Rule 6 and `research-tech.md` Rule 5 — so a turn transition can fire, in the same frame, a turn-change stinger *and* the AP-fill arpeggio *and* the Credit-income cue *and* one (already deduped, per the stacking rule above) completion cue *and*, on a game-ending transition, the victory/defeat cue. Pairwise "louder cue wins" is insufficient; the HUD defines a **single total priority order** the audio owner resolves against, highest first:
>
> **`GameOver (victory/defeat) > turn-change stinger > completion cue (deduped) > Credit-income cue > AP-fill arpeggio`**
>
> - The lower-priority cue in any collision is **ducked** (attenuated under `HUD_AUDIO_DUCK_MS`), except that **`GameOver` hard-preempts** everything below it (no stinger-then-fanfare double-beat — the stinger is cut, not merely ducked, on the game-ending transition, matching the visual overlay's one-frame precedence in Edge Cases).
> - Rationale for the ordering: the disambiguation-critical cues (whose-turn stinger; match-over fanfare) sit above the informational ones (a completion chime, the two start-of-turn additions) so they are never buried under the louder AP/Credit family. The completion cue sits above both start-of-turn additions because a build/research landing is a discrete event the player may have been waiting turns for. Between the two start-of-turn additions, the **Credit-income cue sits above the AP-fill arpeggio** because the growing war chest is the variable, decision-relevant number (it scales with your economy and you're deciding when to spend it), whereas the AP fill is a predictable flat every-turn beat; if the pair must duck one, the flat AP fill yields. Both remain below the completion/disambiguation tiers.
> - `HUD_AUDIO_DUCK_MS` governs the duck window for the *ducked* (non-`GameOver`) collisions; the `GameOver` hard-preempt is a cut, not a duck (see Tuning Knobs note).

### D · Anchor principles & risk

- **HUD-clutter risk (Pillar 3):** the HUD has more simultaneously-visible elements than #9 (which is state-machine-gated to one overlay). Mitigation: strict peripheral/edge zoning (B) — only fixed per-tile readouts touch board interior; the turn banner is the sole transient exception.
- **Neon-overload risk (compounds #9's, and the pivot adds a second persistent counter):** AP counter + Credits counter + faction units + #9 overlays + transient banner/victory can all be true at once — the dual-counter HUD raises the standing neon count by one persistent element. Mitigation: the banner and victory screen are the *only* full-saturation neon the HUD adds beyond the **two** counters, and both are **transient** (and #9 is inert during the banner window per turn-scoping); everything else is neutral chrome. The two counters must sit in **distinct hue families** and share a coherent size/contrast hierarchy so they read as *two* budgets, not neon soup. If either counter reads as "just more clutter," the fix is **size/contrast hierarchy** (make the counters unmistakably the biggest neon elements, co-equal to each other), **not** desaturating them — their neon is Player-Fantasy load-bearing. `/art-bible` must set the AP-vs-Credit hue split as a first-class palette decision.
- **Turn-start simultaneity (the counters must win their own moment):** the AP fill flourish, the Credits income flourish, and the YOUR/ENEMY-TURN banner all fire off the *same* `PlayerTurn` transition and therefore overlap by design — this is the single most Player-Fantasy-load-bearing moment ("my new budget *and* my war chest grew"). The two counters must not merely coexist with the banner; they must **dominate** — and their two simultaneous flourishes must read as two distinct beats (distinct hues + the timbrally-separable AP-fill vs. Credit-income cues, section C) rather than one smeared animation. `/art-bible` / `/ux-design` must set a concrete floor (the two counters are the largest neon elements on screen by a defined size ratio, their contrast against the banner is specified, and their mutual hue separation is fixed) rather than leaving "size/contrast hierarchy" as an unquantified intention. Until that floor exists this is a tracked risk, not a solved one.
- **Late-game legibility is unproven and MUST be playtested (parity with #9):** the "absorb it at a glance" ambient claim (Player Fantasy) is untested against this HUD's own late-game density — up to ~6 on-board glyph classes (hp pips, has-acted, tech marker, structure hp, build-timer badge, research marker) *plus* #9's overlay vocabulary, precisely when the tempo-swing pillar says the board is fullest. The single-unit collision case (Edge Cases) does **not** cover board-scale scanability. #9 mandates a dedicated legibility playtest for a *smaller* vocabulary; this system inherits the same **required deliverable** — a glyph-density/legibility playtest before Production, including a level-of-detail pass for how glyphs read at the zoomed-out "whole board" view. The chess-player analogy in Player Fantasy is aspirational: unlike static chess pieces these readouts are semi-animated, so the real problem is closer to an RTS minimap+resource-bar read and is budgeted as such.
- **Camera model is a silent corpus assumption (a routine fork, NOT a schedule risk — downgraded re-review 2026-07-22):** CR-5's "fixed per-tile sub-position" readouts presuppose a world→screen mapping, but no GDD in the corpus states whether the board camera is **fixed** or can **pan/zoom** (only an incidental "no camera pan/zoom" phrase in #9's visual table). Both answers map to a known, cheap Godot/Redot pattern: fixed → glyphs cached once from `TileMapLayer.map_to_local()` (near-zero per-frame cost); pan/zoom → glyphs parented under the zoomable board root so they auto-scale, or a `CanvasLayer` overlay with a per-frame reproject only if fixed-pixel-size glyphs under zoom are wanted. At a fixed 14×16 board with the "board always fully visible / at-a-glance" framing throughout the corpus, **fixed camera is the near-certain target.** The answer picks between two bounded patterns, not an open-ended unknown. See OQ-8; **Grid & Terrain (#1) owns the board substrate and should confirm the camera model** (recommend fixed as the default). Technical mitigation for the density risk above: extend CR-1's dirty-flag coalescing to the on-board glyph layer (a single coalesced "board overlay" redraw pass), so per-entity glyphs don't each subscribe-and-redraw independently.

### E · Accessibility — non-hue redundancy

- **Has-acted vs. tech vs. normal**: three distinct **glyph shapes** at three fixed tile positions — never color alone, never overlapping the hp pips. **Minimum-size guarantee:** these glyphs must stay **shape-distinguishable at the minimum supported UI scale and at 1440p**, not degrade to indistinguishable dots — the glyph vocabulary needs a legibility floor the way `PIP_MAX_HP` gives hp a numeric fallback. Verified in the legibility playtest (D-risk).
- **YOUR vs. ENEMY turn**: the **load-bearing** non-hue channels are a distinct **text label** + a **directional slide-in** from the owning side; the distinct **audio register is supplementary only** (players mute SFX in long sessions, and colorblind ≠ audio-available are independent populations — audio cannot be the primary fallback).
- **AP / Credits counters — committed vs. projected echo**: on **both** counters the accessible channel is the **`→` arrow + explicit numerals** (`9 → 3`), **not** the "dimmer/desaturated echo of the same hue" (a luminance-only distinction that fails for low-vision / reduced-contrast players). The desaturation is decorative; the arrow-and-numeral structure carries the meaning. Neither counter's first-class-neon legibility may depend on a specific hue that could collide with a faction hue.
- **AP vs. Credits counter distinction**: the two persistent counters must be distinguishable by more than hue alone — a **label/glyph** (AP-labelled vs. a Credit `⛁` glyph) and/or position, not the different hue families by themselves (which fail for colorblind players). The hue distinction is the fast peripheral read; the label/glyph is the accessible fallback.
- **Dual-cost affordability — binding pool**: an unaffordable economic action names the **binding pool** by label + a deny treatment (which of Credits/AP is short), never by a hue-only difference between the two cost fields; greyed-out uses the greyscale/dimmed convention (brightness/saturation, not hue).
- **Victory vs. defeat**: distinct **layout/composition** (bright-upward vs. dimmed-downward) + distinct audio timbre — never a green/red framing alone.
- **Build button affordability**: **greyscale/dimmed** when nothing is affordable (a brightness/saturation cue, not hue) — accessibility-safe by construction; noted here since the audit previously omitted this control.
- **Detail panel — selection vs. inspection**: distinguished by edge treatment + persistence (CR-6), not hue alone.
- **hp pips vs. numeric**: a threshold rule (`PIP_MAX_HP`), not a color distinction — accessibility-neutral by construction.
- **Standing gap (now 3rd recurrence — #6, #9, #10):** faction-hue-as-identity has no shape/pattern fallback; it blocks legibility at the turn banner and victory screen here. **Recommend this become the top-priority item in `/art-bible` Section 2 (faction identity)** — it now gates three independent systems.

### Input notes

- Per `technical-preferences.md`: the two controls (Build, End Turn) must be **click-reachable and keyboard-reachable** so the gamepad/cursor port stays feasible. Every HUD action has a non-hover path (both the Credit income breakdown and the AP-carryover indicator must be reachable by click/press, not hover-only).
- **Dual focus (CONFIRMED present in Redot 26.2 — OQ-5 resolved):** mouse/touch focus is separate from keyboard/gamepad focus, and this is confirmed present in the pinned engine (verified by direct `ClassDB` introspection of the `Redot Engine LTS v26.2.stable` binary during the 2026-07-22 re-review): `Control.grab_click_focus()` (mouse-click focus) and `Control.grab_focus()` (keyboard/gamepad focus) are distinct methods; `Viewport.gui_get_hovered_control()` vs. `gui_get_focus_owner()` are separate queries; the default `Theme` ships distinct `focus` and `hover` StyleBox slots on `Button`. Implementation guidance for the ADR: use the `focus` StyleBox for the keyboard-focus ring and the `hover` StyleBox for mouse-hover (distinct by construction, no custom wiring); use `focus_neighbor_*` / `focus_next` / `focus_previous` for keyboard traversal order; and set `focus_mode = FOCUS_NONE` (or `focus_behavior_recursive` at the container, 4.5+) on present-but-inert controls (CR-10, opponent turn) so a dead button holds no live-looking focus ring — do **not** hand-roll per-frame focus-ring suppression. All five interactive elements (Build, End Turn, Credit-income-breakdown toggle, AP-carryover toggle, action-log scroll) need a **keyboard-focus indicator distinct from mouse-hover**; the concrete traversal *order* across the top-center spine / fixed corner / opposite edge is a UX call owed to `/ux-design` (OQ-5's remaining sub-item — the API to express it is confirmed available).

### Screen regions (summary — full IA in Visual/Audio B)

Top-center spine (AP counter + Credits counter + turn/round); fixed corner (Build + End Turn); opposite edge (action log); edge-docked detail panel; per-tile on-board readouts. **Board center stays clear** — no HUD chrome over the grid interior (Pillar 3).

The **detail-panel content follows #9's selection**; the HUD owns only the panel chrome. These three seams are the entirety of the #9↔#10 interface — the HUD adds no selection, preview, or commit logic of its own.
