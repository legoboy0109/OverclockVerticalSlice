## Overview

The **Faction Identity** system is the data-and-rules layer that makes each playable faction a *distinct way to spend AP and Credits and win tempo* rather than a reskin — the mechanical expression of Pillar 4 ("Factions Are Verbs, Not Skins"). It is not a new gameplay system with its own board presence; it is a **modifier framework** layered over the systems that already own every value it touches (AP & Credits Economy, Unit System, Base & Production, Combat, Research). A faction is defined as a named, data-driven bundle of *modifiers* — deltas to unit costs, income-curve parameters, structure costs/caps, and tech access — applied at match setup and read live by the systems that own those values. Crucially, faction differences **only ever express through the shared AP + Credits economy** (Pillar 1 — "Every Choice Is a Tradeoff"): a faction re-weights the *same two* shared budgets — cheaper units, steeper Credit income, faster structures, different AP surcharges on economic actions — but never introduces a **third resource** or a "free" action outside the shared AP+Credits economy. (The two-budget split itself is Pillar 1's deliberate, bounded exception to "one economy," not an opening for faction-specific currencies — see the Pillar 1 reconciliation note below.) For the Vertical Slice this GDD deliberately specifies the **framework and its plug-in contract**, plus the two seed factions — **Rush/Tempo** (aggressive, cheaper/faster units, weaker economy) and **Boom/Mass** (compounding economy, stronger outposts/tech, slower start) — as *illustrative stubs whose asymmetry values are provisional*. Pillar 4 is empirically unvalidated (the concept prototype was symmetric), so the concrete per-faction numbers are gated on the dedicated faction-asymmetry prototype; this GDD locks *what a faction may modify and how*, not the final balance of *by how much*. Without this system, both sides play identically and the game's headline promise of asymmetric factions has no mechanical basis.

> **Pillar 1 reconciliation (2026-08-05 pivot).** Pillar 1 was reworded from "One Economy, Every Choice" to **"Every Choice Is a Tradeoff"** (`game-concept.md`) when the single AP pool split into two budgets — flat tactical **AP** and banked **Credits** — with a small AP surcharge keeping every Credits investment tempo-linked. Pillar 1's anti-pillar is explicit that the game is "not a sprawl of parallel currencies": AP + Credits are *the* deliberate, bounded exception, not a precedent for more. This GDD's constraint updates to match — a faction may re-weight the shared AP+Credits economy (including, in principle, a faction's AP surcharge on economic actions — the framework allows it; no VS faction uses it, see Interactions table), but it may never add a **third** resource or a 0-cost action outside AP+Credits. This is the same invariant the prior single-pool wording enforced, restated for two budgets instead of one (see CR-3).

> **Provisional-framework marker (framework v1).** This GDD locks *what a faction may modify and how* — not the balance of *by how much*. The one framework assumption it cannot self-validate is that **tempo/quantity-only asymmetry** (CR-6 locks combat stats to identity) is *sufficient* to deliver Pillar 4; that sufficiency is prototype-gated (OQ-9). **Named reopen-trigger:** *if the faction-asymmetry prototype finds tempo-only asymmetry insufficient to make Rush and Boom read as distinct factions, OQ-9 reopens the CR-6 combat-stat lock — a framework-shape change requiring a fresh `/design-review`, not a balance-tuning pass.* Approving this GDD approves the framework as the substrate the prototype runs against; it does not pre-judge that finding.

## Player Fantasy

> *Lean review mode: `creative-director` not consulted for this section — drafted from the game concept's Core Fantasy, Pillar 4, and the rush/boom archetype seed. Flag for a pass before production if faction feel diverges in playtest.*

**The feeling: "this faction wages war *my* way."**

OVERCLOCK's core fantasy is mastery of tempo; Faction Identity's job is to let a player pick the *shape* of that mastery and feel it in every AP they spend. Choosing the **Rush/Tempo** faction should feel like choosing to live on the front foot — your units come cheap and fast, your economy is thin, and the whole faction leans your instincts toward "hit before they spike." Choosing **Boom/Mass** should feel like choosing patience with teeth — your early turns are lean and a little anxious, but your economy compounds and your late-game overwhelms. The faction is not a costume over identical play; it is a *pre-commitment to a way of spending the shared AP + Credits economy*, and a skilled player should feel that commitment paying off (or being punished) in the tempo duel.

Two audiences this serves directly: the **Competitor** who wants their strategic personality (aggressor vs. accumulator) to be a real mechanical stance, not a flavor label; and the **Explorer** who wants to *learn a faction's distinct AP rhythm and opening* — the satisfaction of discovering "the Rush opening" versus "the Boom opening" as genuinely different puzzles. The failure mode this system exists to prevent is the Pillar 4 anti-pattern: two factions that play the same with different neon hues. If a player can swap factions and keep the same opening, this system has failed its fantasy.

For the Vertical Slice, "distinct" does not yet mean "deeply tuned." Because the asymmetry is prototype-gated, the fantasy target here is *directional* — the two seed factions should already read as "aggressor" and "accumulator" in feel — with the precise dial settings that make each satisfying deferred to the asymmetry prototype and playtest.

**Accepted boundary (stated, so the framework does not over-claim):** CR-6 locks combat stats (hp/atk/range) to identity for all VS factions, so every bit of Rush/Boom asymmetry the VS can express is a **tempo/quantity** difference (cheaper, faster, sooner, more) — never a **per-unit-quality** difference (Rush's units are not individually weaker, Boom's not individually stronger). Words like "teeth" and "overwhelms" above therefore mean *what arrives, when, and how much* — Boom's late-game overwhelms by fielding **more** of the same-strength army its economy affords, and Rush hits first by fielding a cheaper army **sooner** — not by any unit biting harder. This is a genuine "way to play" difference (a tempo asymmetry is a verb difference, not a reskin — cf. early-spam vs. late-deathball economies), and it is a deliberate scope choice to keep deterministic combat (Pillar 2) untouched. But it is a **real narrowing** of the asymmetry surface: whether tempo-only asymmetry is *sufficient* to make Rush and Boom feel like distinct factions (Pillar 4) is exactly what the asymmetry prototype must validate before combat-stat deltas are ever unlocked (OQ-9). The closed domain list (CR-2) is the *framework*, not a claim that it is already *sufficient* to deliver Pillar 4 — that sufficiency is unproven and prototype-gated.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

**CR-1 — A faction is data, not logic.** A faction is a named, statically-typed data resource (`FactionDef`) — a bundle of *modifier values* with no executable behaviour of its own. It is loaded at match setup and never contains code paths, only numbers and enum flags. This keeps factions data-driven (project coding standard) and lets designers add/tune factions without touching system logic.

**CR-2 — Closed set of modifiable domains (the framework contract).** A `FactionDef` may modify *only* the following, and nothing else. This closed list **is** the framework this GDD locks:
1. **Unit cost deltas** — additive AP delta to each unit type's `produce_cost` (Unit System owns the base).
2. **Unit mobility/stat deltas** — additive delta to a unit type's `move_cost` and/or `soft_move_cap` (Unit System owns the base). *Combat stats (hp/atk/range) are modifiable in the schema but locked to identity for the VS seed factions — see CR-6.*
3. **Income-curve parameters** — additive deltas to `BASE_INCOME` and to the outpost income tier bonuses, now modifying **`credit_income`** (AP & Credits Economy owns the base formula).
4. **Structure cost / build-time / cap deltas** — additive deltas to each structure's `build_cost`, `build_time`, and `production_cap` (Base & Production owns the base). *A faction `production_cap` delta only moves a cap **within** the producing range: it may reduce but never zero a base-positive cap (floored at 1), and it may never raise a base-**zero** cap above 0 (a non-producer structure — Research Lab / Defensive Structure, `base-production.md` `production_cap = 0` — stays a non-producer for every faction). See Formulas 4c and CR-3: a faction re-costs a verb, it neither asymmetrically deletes one nor manufactures one the base game withholds.*
5. **Tech access & cost deltas** — per-tech "available? / `research_cost` delta / `research_time` delta" (Research owns the base). *Design intent (stated, not accidental): the `available?` boolean is the **one deliberately-binary lever** in CR-2 — it is the sanctioned "big" identity lever for headline faction shape (e.g. "Rush cannot research Economy"), unlike the five continuous re-costing levers. A hard tech-denial IS a permitted asymmetry because a tech is an **optional unlock the base game never grants for free** (denying it removes an *addition*, not a *base verb every player starts able to do*) — this is why it is treated differently from a `production_cap` verb-deletion (which removes a base capability and is therefore floored, see 4c). Because it is the strongest single lever, any faction using `tech_available = false` is flagged for a deliberate-intent per-faction design review — see OQ-5.*
6. **Starting loadout** — starting AP, starting units, and starting structures placed at setup (Game State & Turn Manager owns setup).

**CR-3 — Pillar 1 invariant (hard constraint).** A faction may only *re-cost, re-time, or re-scale actions that are already priced in AP and/or Credits*. It may **never** (a) add a **third** resource, (b) grant an action that costs 0 AP/0 Credits where the base action costs AP/Credits, (c) introduce a new verb the base game lacks, or (d) modify a value not in CR-2's closed list. Any proposed faction feature failing this test is cut or re-expressed as an AP and/or Credits modifier. This is the mechanical enforcement of "no third resource" — the two-budget AP+Credits split (Pillar 1's bounded exception to "one economy") is the shared substrate every faction re-weights, not a precedent for faction-specific currencies.

**CR-4 — Live modifier resolution (no base mutation).** Faction modifiers are applied *at the point each owning system reads its value*, exactly like Research tech bonuses feed `effective_attack` — the faction never rewrites base data. Each consuming system exposes an "effective" lookup that folds in the acting player's faction delta, e.g. `effective_produce_cost(unit, player) = max(1, base_produce_cost(unit) + faction_of(player).unit_cost_delta[unit])`. The base registry values are untouched; the faction delta is one more input to the effective value, floored so no cost/stat goes below its legal minimum (≥1 AP for costs, ≥0 for stats).

**CR-5 — Symmetric framework, asymmetric content.** Every faction draws from the *same* modifier categories (CR-2) — the framework is symmetric. Asymmetry lives entirely in the *values*. Both players select a faction at setup; mirror matches (same faction both sides) are legal and are the natural balance baseline.

**CR-6 — VS ships a Neutral baseline + two provisional seed factions.** The Vertical Slice defines exactly three `FactionDef`s: **Neutral** (all modifiers at identity — the current default and the symmetric reference the corpus is already balanced around), **Rush/Tempo**, and **Boom/Mass**. Rush and Boom's modifier *values are provisional and prototype-gated* (Pillar 4 unvalidated); until the faction-asymmetry prototype validates them, matches default to Neutral-vs-Neutral so the VS remains playable and balanced on the values every other Approved GDD assumes. Combat-stat modifiers (CR-2.2) stay at identity for all three VS factions — the seed asymmetry is expressed through cost/economy/tempo only, keeping the deterministic combat math (Pillar 2) untouched for now.

**CR-7 — Determinism.** A `FactionDef` is fixed data applied identically every match; there is no per-match randomization of faction values (Pillar 2 / anti-luck). Given the same two factions and the same play, matches are reproducible.

### States and Transitions

A player's faction has a short setup-phase lifecycle — previewed and acknowledged in the picker, committed once, then immutable for the match. The **SELECTING** sub-state exists specifically so the pre-lock preview (AC-28) and experimental-acknowledgment (AC-27) contracts have a state to live in *before* the commit that places the starting loadout (AC-26) — without it, "picked" and "committed" would collapse into a single transition and the two UI contracts would have nowhere to attach:

| From state | Trigger | To state | Side effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UNASSIGNED** | Match setup begins — the faction picker opens for the player (or campaign/skirmish config) | SELECTING | None yet — no `faction_of(player)` set, no loadout placed |
| **SELECTING** | Player highlights/selects a candidate `FactionDef` in the picker | SELECTING | Candidate faction's starting loadout (CR-2.6) is **previewable** (AC-28); **no** board placement occurs yet. Re-highlighting a different faction stays in SELECTING (pre-lock re-pick — whether re-picking is allowed and whether it re-triggers the acknowledgment is governed by OQ-8) |
| **SELECTING** | Player **confirms** the pick — and, for a non-Neutral (provisional) faction, first passes the experimental-values acknowledgment (AC-27); Neutral requires no acknowledgment | ASSIGNED | `faction_of(player)` set; starting loadout (CR-2.6) placed (AC-26) |
| **ASSIGNED** | Turn Manager transitions Setup → first PlayerTurn | LOCKED | Faction is now immutable for the match; all effective-value lookups fold in its modifiers |
| **LOCKED** | — | — | No transition — no mid-match faction switching exists (would violate the "pre-commitment" fantasy and determinism) |

> The picker never places anything until the **SELECTING → ASSIGNED** confirm transition. Highlighting a faction only previews it (AC-28); the acknowledgment gate (AC-27) sits *on* the confirm transition for non-Neutral factions. This keeps AC-26's "loadout placed at UNASSIGNED→ASSIGNED" honest — placement fires exactly once, at commit, never during preview.

### Interactions with Other Systems

Faction Identity is a pure *modifier provider*: it owns the modifier values and the schema; each consuming system owns its base value and applies the delta at its own read site (CR-4). Under the VS's Neutral default, every delta is identity, so **no consuming system's shipped numbers change** — the handoffs below are additive "fold in an optional faction delta (no-op at identity)" contracts.

| System | What faction modifies | Who applies it | VS status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Game State & Turn Manager** (#2) | Holds `faction_of(player)`; applies starting loadout at setup | Turn Manager (setup) | Owed: store faction per player + apply starting loadout |
| **AP & Credits Economy** (#3) | `BASE_INCOME` + outpost tier bonuses (income-curve params), now folding into `credit_income`; framework also allows a faction to re-weight AP surcharges on economic actions (CR-2 does not currently list this as a separate domain — no VS faction uses it, kept Neutral-inert) | AP & Credits Economy's `credit_income` | Owed: optional faction term, identity under Neutral |
| **Unit System** (#4) | `produce_cost`, `move_cost`, `soft_move_cap` (+ combat stats, identity-locked for VS) | Unit's effective-value lookups | Owed: `effective_produce_cost`/`effective_move_cost` fold in faction delta |
| **Base & Production** (#7) | Structure `build_cost` / `build_time` / `production_cap` | Base & Production | Owed: effective structure-cost lookups |
| **Research/Tech** (#8) | Per-tech availability, `research_cost`, `research_time` | Research | Owed: effective research-cost + availability gate |
| **Combat Resolution** (#6) | (Combat stats only — identity-locked for VS) | Combat via Unit's effective stats | No change in VS (stat deltas at identity) |
| **AI Opponent** (#11) | Reads `faction_of(player)` for per-faction scoring weights | AI Opponent | Out of scope (AI OQ-6 — future) |

**Ownership note:** Faction is a *consumer/provider of modifiers*, never an owner of base values or formulas — the same "holds no copy of a balance constant it can read live" discipline the AI Opponent and Command & Action Interface follow.

## Formulas

> *Lean review mode: drafted by `systems-designer`. Framework-level only — no Rush/Boom balance numbers (those are prototype-gated). Defines the modifier-resolution formula family and its clamps.*

**Generic modifier-resolution formula.** Every faction effect resolves through one shape, applied at the owning system's read site (CR-4):

```
effective_X(base_owner, player) =
    clamp(base_X(base_owner) + faction_delta_X(faction_of(player), base_owner), floor_X, ceiling_X)
```

| Symbol | Type | Range | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| `base_X` | int/float | domain-specific | Value already Approved in the owning GDD — read-only, never mutated |
| `faction_delta_X` | int | schema-unbounded; **always 0 for Neutral** | The faction's additive modifier for this `(domain, base_owner)` pair |
| `floor_X` / `ceiling_X` | int/∞ | domain-specific | Legal min/max (per-domain table). **Note:** across all VS domains only the **floor** ever binds — every per-domain instantiation below is `max(floor, base+Δ)` with `ceiling_X = ∞`. The `ceiling_X` half of the generic form is currently **dead** (no domain uses it) and is retained only as a placeholder for a future domain that might need an upper clamp (e.g. a capped `production_cap`); it is **not** the income-ceiling guard — that is the separate combined-ceiling *validation rule*, not a per-query clamp. Domains 5c (`tech_available`, a boolean gate) and 6 (starting loadout, a replacement set) are **not clamp-shaped at all** and only share this table for locality. |
| `effective_X` | int/float | `[floor_X, ceiling_X]` | What every consuming system's read site actually uses |

Mirrors `effective_attack`/`effective_defense` (`base + delta`, additive, floored). **Additive, not multiplicative** — integer-AP is a hard invariant (a ×0.8 on Scout cost 2 = 1.6 breaks it); if a future faction ever needs scaling, it overrides a *rate constant* (the `SOFT_MOVE_PENALTY`/`CANCEL_REFUND_RATE` precedent), never bolts a multiplier onto an additive delta.

**Per-domain instantiation (floors + rationale):**

| Domain | Effective formula | Floor | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. `produce_cost` | `max(1, base + Δ)` | 1 AP | 0/negative = free verb (CR-3 violation) |
| 2a. `move_cost` | `max(MIN_MOVE_COST, base + Δ)` | 1 AP/tile | 0-cost move = free verb + unbounded Dijkstra reachability |
| 2b. `soft_move_cap` | `max(1, base + Δ)` | 1 tile | 0 collapses the base/surcharge two-tier shape |
| 3. `credit_income` (was `ap_income`) | `max(BASE_INCOME_FLOOR, (BASE_INCOME+Δ_base) + (OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER1+Δ_tier1)·min(n,T) + (OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER2+Δ_tier2)·max(0,n−T) + econ_tech_term)` — where **`econ_tech_term = has_economy_tech(player) ? ECONOMY_TECH_INCOME_BONUS·min(n, ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD) : 0`**, carried **verbatim** from AP & Credits Economy's *current 4-term* formula (`ap-economy.md`) — only the resource it denominates changed (Credits, not AP). Factions do **not** modify the tech term or its `ECONOMY_TECH_TIER_THRESHOLD` (=6) cap. | `BASE_INCOME_FLOOR` | Rule-7's floor (10) assumes no subtractive term; a lower floor keeps income > 0. **The base folded here is AP & Credits Economy's *complete, current* `credit_income` formula — the Economy-Tech term and its tier cap are load-bearing (they close a real unbounded-income defect AP & Credits Economy already paid to fix) and must not be dropped when a faction income delta is applied. Domain-3's two sub-levers are named separately below (intercept vs slope).** |
| 4a. `build_cost` | `max(1, base + Δ)` | 1 AP | free-verb logic |
| 4b. `build_time` | `max(MIN_BUILD_TIME, base + Δ)` | 1 turn | instant build removes the vulnerable-investment window B&P documents as intentional |

(b) *Symmetrically*, a faction may **not** raise a base-**zero** cap above 0 — a structure the base game defines as a non-producer (Research Lab / Defensive Structure, `base-production.md` `production_cap = 0`) cannot be turned into a producer for one faction, because that *creates a new verb the base game withholds* (CR-3 (c)). CR-2.4's delta authority is thus bounded on both ends. The Research-Lab cap-0 is a *symmetric* base-game property (both players), never a faction lever. |
| 5a. `research_cost` | `max(1, base + Δ)` | 1 AP | free-verb logic |
| 5b. `research_time` | `max(MIN_RESEARCH_TIME, base + Δ)` | 1 turn | instant research breaks the destroy-while-researching interrupt |
| 5c. `tech_available` | `base_available AND faction_allows(faction, tech)` | (boolean gate) | availability is a gate, not a clamp |
| 6. starting loadout | `starting_state(player) = faction.starting_loadout` | `starting_ap ≥ 0` | a *replacement set*, not a delta; every placed entity must already be a legal priced entity elsewhere (CR-3) |

**Worked example — Neutral (the critical invariant):** every `Δ = 0` ⇒ `effective_X = clamp(base_X + 0, floor, ∞) = base_X`, since every Approved value already sits at/above its floor (e.g. Scout `effective_produce_cost = max(1, 2+0) = 2`). **Confirms CR-6: Neutral-vs-Neutral changes nothing in any Approved GDD.**

**Worked example — illustrative boundary stress (NOT a balance proposal):** a hypothetical Δ = −3 on Scout `produce_cost` ⇒ `max(1, 2−3) = 1` — clamp absorbs cleanly; Δ = −5 on Heavy `soft_move_cap` (base 2) ⇒ `max(1, 2−5) = 1`.

**Income domain — two named sub-levers (framework contract, not a value).** CR-2.3's "income-curve parameters" is **two distinct levers** on `credit_income`, and they must be named because they shape the felt economic *arc* differently, independent of any value chosen:
- **Intercept lever (`Δ_base_income`)** — shifts the whole curve up/down by a constant. Felt maximally at turn 1, compresses toward noise as income climbs (a flat −2 is 20% of early base-10 income but under 8% of a late ~26–32). This is the right lever for a **uniform-from-turn-1** trait (e.g. Rush's economic *penalty*: bad at economy always).
- **Slope levers (`Δ_tier1` / `Δ_tier2`)** — change the *per-outpost* marginal bonus, so the gap widens as the economy scales. This is the right lever for a **compounding** trait (e.g. Boom's economy that "feels increasingly dominant late"). An intercept lever alone *structurally cannot* deliver a compounding arc no matter what value is chosen — a shape fact the asymmetry prototype must respect, not discover.

**Combined-income-ceiling rule (hard framework constraint, load-bearing before any non-Neutral income delta ships).** Because a faction income delta stacks a *third* per-`n` term onto a base curve that already multiplies through `OUTPOST_BONUS_TIER1/TIER2` **and** the Economy-Tech term, the same unbounded-stacking failure AP & Credits Economy already caught once (untiered Economy-Tech term, ceiling 26→38 before a cap was retrofitted) can recur along the faction axis. Therefore: **any faction carrying a non-zero income delta (`Δ_base`/`Δ_tier1`/`Δ_tier2`) must be validated against the same `n`-swept ceiling model AP & Credits Economy used in its re-review, and the *combined* ceiling (base tiers × Economy Tech × faction delta) must be re-approved by economy-designer as a single number — not three independently-approved deltas.** This is a validation-discipline rule that costs nothing under Neutral (all Δ = 0, so the combined ceiling *is* AP & Credits Economy's Approved ~26/~32 `credit_income`) and is owed the moment a real delta lands. See OQ-3.

**Two framework flags (prototype-gated, surfaced not resolved):**
- **Per-stat saturation (Pillar 4 risk):** some base values already sit at their floor (Economy Outpost `build_time` = 1), so no faction delta can shorten them — only lengthen. And two factions with deltas −2 and −5 on the same floored stat become *indistinguishable* on that stat. **This is not abstract: run against the actual Approved roster, the two most identity-defining data points are already floored** — Scout `produce_cost` = 2 (a Rush discount can only reach the 1-AP floor: one point of range on the roster's signature cheap unit) and Economy Outpost `build_time` = 1 (no faction can make Boom's signature structure complete *faster* than Neutral). The asymmetry prototype must run a **per-stat headroom audit against the real Approved values** (not per-faction averages) before setting any delta — several levers have near-zero usable range where the fantasy needs them most.
- **Additive income compresses over the match:** covered by the intercept/slope split above — a flat `Δ_base_income` compresses late; the slope levers are the compounding-safe alternative. A shape choice the framework now names explicitly, not a value left to tuning.

**Floors — one already exists today, the rest are one-line additions owed to owning GDDs:**
- **`MIN_MOVE_COST` is NOT undocumented and NOT faction-deferred** — Movement System's Approved GDD already requires `move_cost ≥ 1` *today* as a monotonicity precondition for its Dijkstra reachability shortcut (`movement-system.md`), independent of Faction Identity. Domain 2a simply *reuses* that existing floor (`MIN_MOVE_COST` = Movement's `move_cost ≥ 1`); nothing new is owed here.
- **Genuinely owed (one-line additions to the owning docs, load-bearing only once a faction carries a subtractive delta):** `BASE_INCOME_FLOOR` (AP & Credits Economy, applies to `credit_income` — needs economy-designer sign-off), `MIN_BUILD_TIME` (Base & Production), `MIN_RESEARCH_TIME` (Research/Tech). *(The former `production_cap ≥ 0` item is superseded — faction deltas now floor at 1, see Formulas 4c; the mechanical `≥ 0` remains Base & Production's existing property, not a new owed floor.)*

## Edge Cases

- **If no faction is assigned to a player at setup:** default to **Neutral** (all deltas identity). Guarantees every match is valid and playable on the corpus's shipped balance — the VS default is Neutral-vs-Neutral.
- **If both players pick the same faction (mirror match):** fully legal; it is the intended balance baseline (symmetric, so any imbalance is the map/first-move, not the faction).
- **If two players pick *different* factions (asymmetric match):** each player's effective-value lookups read *their own* `faction_of(player)` deltas — `effective_X` takes `player` as an argument, so there is no cross-contamination (player A's Rush discount never touches player B's units).
- **If a faction delta would drive a value below its floor:** the Formulas clamp applies (e.g. `max(1, …)`). The result is legal; the excess magnitude is silently absorbed — see the per-stat saturation flag (two factions can become indistinguishable on a floored stat; the asymmetry prototype must check this).
- **If a faction's starting loadout would place an entity on an illegal tile** (occupied, off-board, or violating Base & Production's placement rules): setup validation rejects the loadout — faction loadouts must be *authored* to place only on legal setup tiles for the map. A malformed loadout is a data error caught at load, not a runtime state.
- **If a faction marks a tech unavailable (`faction_allows` false) and the player attempts to research it:** Research's availability gate blocks it exactly as it blocks any illegal action — the tech simply never appears as a legal research target for that faction.
- **If a faction delta would drive a base-positive producer's `production_cap` to 0 (or below):** the faction clamp floors it at **1**, not 0 (Formulas 4c / CR-2.4) — a faction can make a producer *slower* (lower cap) but can never fully delete its production verb, because that would be an asymmetric verb-removal (CR-3's spirit).
- **If a faction delta targets a base-cap-0 structure** (a non-producer such as the Research Lab or Defensive Structure, `base-production.md` `production_cap = 0`): the delta is **inert** — the effective cap stays 0, because a faction may not turn a base-game non-producer into a producer (that would manufacture a verb the base game withholds, CR-3 (c)). Together these two cases mean a faction `production_cap` delta only moves a cap *within* the producing range and never crosses the produces/doesn't-produce boundary in either direction — **resolving** the earlier CR-3-borderline concern by rule rather than by per-faction review. (A true cap of 0 remains a *symmetric* base-game structure property — both players affected identically.)
- **If a base entity a faction's delta keys to is later removed or renamed** (a future Approved-GDD change): the delta is keyed by `(domain, base_owner)`; an orphaned delta is inert and ignored, and schema validation warns at load. Factions never hard-break on a base-roster change — they degrade to "no modifier for the missing entity."
- **If the AI Opponent (#11) plays a non-Neutral faction:** it reads the same `effective_X` queries every other consumer does (CR-4), so its costs/income are automatically faction-correct with *zero* AI code change. However, its *scoring weights* are not faction-tuned in the VS (AI OQ-6) — so the AI plays a faction "correctly costed but with Neutral strategy," an acceptable VS limitation flagged for post-VS.
- **If a faction is switched mid-match:** impossible by construction — faction is LOCKED at the Setup→PlayerTurn transition (States) and immutable thereafter. There is no code path to change it, so no mid-match-switch edge case can arise.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

**N/A for the mechanical layer; cross-references only.** The Faction Identity *modifier framework* has no presentation surface of its own — it outputs numbers, not pixels or sound. Faction *identity* is strongly visual in OVERCLOCK, but those requirements are owned elsewhere: (1) **Faction = hue** — each faction owns a saturated neon hue for at-a-glance ownership (art bible Visual Identity Anchor, Principle 2; Pillar 3). The framework requires only that each shipped `FactionDef` declares a `faction_hue` handle the renderer keys tint off; the hue values and palette are the art bible's call. (2) Silhouette-first units (Principle 1) are unit-art concerns, not faction-framework concerns. No faction-specific SFX in the VS; a per-faction audio motif may be worth a pass once the asymmetry prototype validates the seed factions (deferred).

> **Schema contract — non-hue identity fallback (closes a 4×-recurring corpus gap).** Because **this GDD owns the `FactionDef` schema** (every other doc — Combat, Command & Action Interface, Game HUD — only *consumes* `faction_hue`), it is the correct place to foreclose the colorblind gap those three docs each independently flagged: faction-hue-as-identity has no confirmed shape/pattern fallback. The `FactionDef` schema therefore declares a **second, reserved identity handle `faction_pattern_id`** alongside `faction_hue` — a non-hue redundant identifier (glyph/pattern/insignia) every consumer keys off *in addition to* hue. The **value/asset is deferred to `/art-bible`** (this GDD sets no pattern art), but reserving the field now means no downstream consumer ships an hue-only lookup path that needs a later retrofit. Verified by AC-6b.

## UI Requirements

**One UI surface owed: faction selection at match setup.** The framework requires a *faction picker* on the skirmish/setup screen (and a campaign config hook) so each player's `FactionDef` is chosen before the Setup→PlayerTurn lock (States). The picker must present the 3 VS factions and default to Neutral. Full *visual* design defers to `/ux-design` (OQ-8), but the following **interaction/data contracts are binding now** (they are framework requirements, not visual polish, so they must not be hand-waved):

- **Experimental factions are gated behind an explicit acknowledgment, not merely labelled.** Because Rush/Boom balance values are provisional/unvalidated, selecting a non-Neutral faction requires the player to pass an explicit "these values are unvalidated" acknowledgment step before the pick can lock. (Neutral — the balanced default — needs no such step.) This is a *behavioral* requirement the UX pass must implement, not a "make it look experimental" suggestion.
- **Starting loadout is previewable before lock-in.** A faction may carry a distinct starting loadout (CR-2.6) — the single largest commitment a player makes per match. Per the corpus's established *preview-before-commit* precedent (Command & Action Interface previews cost/targets before any in-match action), the picker MUST let the player preview a faction's starting units/structures/AP before the Setup→PlayerTurn lock. Committing to a faction without seeing what it starts with would silently violate that precedent.
- **Faction-reveal sequencing in skirmish-vs-AI and pre-lock re-picking** are stated as UX-pass questions (OQ-8): whether the AI's faction is shown before or after the player locks (governs whether counter-picking is possible — a real strategic axis for an asymmetric-faction game), and whether re-picking is allowed pre-lock and whether it re-triggers the experimental acknowledgment. These are flagged for `/ux-design` to resolve, not left silent.

This is a HUD/UX concern, not owned here beyond the binding contracts above. In-match there is no faction-specific UI beyond the existing HUD showing each side's neon hue (and its `faction_pattern_id` fallback).

> **📌 UX Flag — Faction Identity**: This system has a UI surface (the setup-screen faction picker, OQ-8). In Pre-Production, run `/ux-design` for a faction-select screen **before** writing epics. Stories that reference faction selection should cite `design/ux/[screen].md`, not this GDD.
