# Turning cover on, and teaching the AI to use it

> **Follows**: `s7-10-cliff-tuning-2026-08-25.md`, which found that cover was fully built and
> never placed on any map
> **Date**: 2026-08-25 · **Story**: S7-11
> **Brief**: *"turn on cover and teach the AI to use it"*

---

## What was already there

Cover needed **no new mechanics**. All of it shipped in the Foundation sprints and has been
inert ever since:

| Piece | Where | State before S7-11 |
|---|---|---|
| Damage reduction | `CombatConfig.cover_dr = 1` | Live, never triggered |
| Terrain query | `GridState.is_cover()` | Live, never true |
| Rendering | `BoardRenderer` cover props + `tile_cover_clean.png` | Live, never spawned |
| Cover-immunity for structures | combat-resolution Rule 6 | Live |
| **A map that places a cover tile** | — | ⛔ **Did not exist** |

★ Both the slice and the simulator filled their boards with `Terrain.PLAIN`. So did both
diagnostic tools. **Four hand-built copies of the same map, agreeing only because they were all
trivially empty.**

## What changed

**1. One authored map — `src/core/map/vs_map.gd`.** The four duplicates now call `VSMap.build()`.
This matters beyond tidiness: `simulate_matches.gd`'s own header demands it mirror the slice,
and that promise was kept only by everything being blank. The moment terrain existed, it would
have quietly stopped being true.

**2. Cover placed — 8 tiles of 120 (~7%), mirror-symmetric, clear of both deploy rings.**

**3. The AI learned to value it.** Only one half needed code:

- **Attack scoring already accounted for cover, and always had.** `_consider_attack` values a
  target through `Combat.preview_damage`, which applies `cover_dr` — a defender in cover has
  always been a less attractive target. A test now pins that, so nobody adds a second term and
  double-counts it.
- **Move scoring did not.** `AIConfig.cover_value = 0.30` is added to `_positional_value` and
  `_retreat_value` when the destination is cover. Sized deliberately between one tile of
  progress (0.16) and a next-turn attack setup (0.4), and well under `lethal_floor_bonus`.

## ★★ Two things that were measured and came out backwards

### The "let a unit trade a tile of progress for cover" idea makes cover usage *worse*

The obvious follow-up — let the advance fold accept one tile less progress to end in cover —
was implemented and tested:

| AI | Units standing in cover |
|---|---:|
| Cover-blind (incidental only) | 5.5 % |
| `cover_value` only | **6.1 %** |
| `cover_value` + one-tile discount | **4.7 %** ⛔ worse than blind |

★ **A unit that steps back into cover is further from the enemy, so next turn it advances and
immediately leaves.** The discount bought more time walking, not more time protected. **Cover
rewards a defender who stays put, and this AI never stays put** — every branch of its movement
scoring is advance, siege or retreat. The knob is kept at 0 with the finding written next to it,
because it becomes correct the moment a hold-position behaviour exists.

### Cover in the lane both sides must cross turns close games into draws

The first layout used **14 tiles including the centre pair on the HQ-to-HQ rank**. It broke the
S6-06 gate in a very specific place:

| Layout | Resolve on play | +1 handicap cell |
|---|---:|---|
| No cover (S7-10) | **19/22** | 1/6 reached the round cap |
| 14 tiles, centre lane covered | **15/22** | **5/6 reached the round cap** |
| **8 tiles, lane open (shipped)** | **17/22** | 3/6 reached the round cap |

★ Handicaps +2 and +3 were untouched at 25–30 turns in all three. **The damage was entirely in
the nearly-even games.** Cover let a slightly-behind player hold — which is the intent — and then
kept holding, so the game stopped being a comeback and became a stalemate.

> ⇒ **Cover belongs where a player chooses to fight, not where they are forced to.** The direct
> lane between the HQs is now deliberately open ground.

## ⚠ Honest limits

- **The AI uses cover, but lightly** — 4.7 % of unit-turns, against ~7 % of the board being
  cover. It is an aggressive agent that is nearly always moving; cover pays a defender who
  holds ground. **Making it a genuinely cover-aware tactician needs a hold-position behaviour,
  which is a real AI feature and not a weight.**
- **The player benefits far more than the AI does**, and that is not measured here at all. A
  human can choose to sit in cover; this AI structurally cannot.
- ★ **The round cap is now the binding constraint on close games.** `VS_MAX_ROUNDS` is 30 (≈60
  turns) and was calibrated before both the S7-10 cooldown change and cover. Five of the seven
  unresolved games are +0 or +1 — the close cells. **Raising the cap is the obvious next lever
  and is a design call**: it would convert stalemates into finishes at the cost of longer matches.
- Cover density (8 tiles) and `cover_dr` (1) are both unexplored beyond this one comparison.
