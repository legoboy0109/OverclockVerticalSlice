# Raising the round cap — and the seat bias it uncovered

> **Follows**: `s7-11-cover-2026-08-25.md`, which found the cap had become the binding
> constraint on close games
> **Date**: 2026-08-25 · **Story**: S7-12
> **Change**: `VerticalSliceRoot.VS_MAX_ROUNDS` **30 → 40**

---

## Why

30 rounds was calibrated in Sprint 5, when unit counts stabilised by round 20–30. **Two later
changes lengthened matches and neither re-derived it:**

- **S7-10** — faster HQ reinforcement, so bigger armies and longer engagements
- **S7-11** — cover, a flat −1 damage, so every exchange takes more hits

By S7-11 the cap was deciding games that should have been decided on play, and it was doing so
*selectively*: the nearly-even +1 cell had 3 of 6 games ending on the cap, while +2 and +3
finished comfortably at 25–30 turns. **The cap was truncating exactly the games worth watching.**

## Result

| Round cap | Resolve on play | +1 cell reaching the cap | Mean turns | Longest |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **30** (was) | 17/22 | **3/6** | 33.2 | 60 |
| **40** (shipped) | **20/22** | **0/6** | 38.7 | 80 |

✅ **Every handicapped game now finishes on play.** The two remaining unresolved games are both
in the mirror cell, which is correct rather than residual — a deterministic AI on a symmetric
board *should* tiebreak sometimes.

⚠ **Cost:** mean match length 33 → 39 turns, and a capped game now runs 80 turns instead of 60.

## ⛔ What raising the cap uncovered — read this before trusting the conversion number

The gate's "material advantage converts" figure moved **18/18 → 15/18**. That looks like the
comeback the earlier sprints were hunting for. **It is not.**

Every one of the three "upsets" is the same game:

```
+1 favoured=P0 var0: winner P1 in 70 turns   ★ upset
+1 favoured=P0 var1: winner P1 in 70 turns   ★ upset
+1 favoured=P0 var2: winner P1 in 70 turns   ★ upset
+1 favoured=P1 var0: winner P1 in 46 turns   converts
+1 favoured=P1 var1: winner P1 in 46 turns   converts
+1 favoured=P1 var2: winner P1 in 30 turns   converts
```

**P1 wins all six +1 games regardless of which side was given the extra Trooper.** It also wins
all four mirror games. ★ **In close games the winner is the seat, not the play.**

### It is not turn order, and it is not the faction

- **Not turn order.** The mirror cell alternates the starting player by variant, and P1 wins
  whether it moves first or second.
- **Not the faction.** `rush.tres`, `boom.tres` and `neutral.tres` all carry `unit_deltas = []`
  — the faction framework is built and inert, so the two sides are mechanically identical.
- **Not the cover layout.** Tile-level mirror symmetry is pinned by
  `tests/unit/map/vs_map_test.gd`, and the *resolved* mirror games go to P1 on a plain board
  too (`--plain --max-rounds=40`: P1 wins both games that finish on play).

### What cover did change

On a plain board the mirror cell splits **2/2**; with cover it is **4/0 to P1**. The difference
is entirely in the two games that reach the cap and are decided by the unit-count **tiebreak** —
plain gives those to P0, cover gives them to P1. So cover did not create the bias; it moved
which side wins a tiebreak.

> ⇒ **The bias predates all of this.** S5-04 saw it at n=4 and recorded it as "a signal, not a
> proof" — *"the player who moves first loses every mirror"*. With more games and a cap that
> lets them finish, it resolves into something sharper and worse: **P1 wins close games, full
> stop.** Raising the cap did not cause it. It stopped hiding it.

## ⇒ What this means for the numbers already recorded

- **S6-06's "18/18 material advantage converts" was partly the cap.** At 30 rounds the games
  that would have been lost by the favoured side were truncated and awarded on unit count.
- **S7-09's and S7-10's exchange-rate sweeps used the +1 cell**, which is the cell most affected.
  Their *relative* comparisons (shipped vs tuned, measured under identical conditions) still
  hold; their absolute framing of "one Trooper ≈ N% of decision quality" now carries an
  asterisk, because a seat effect sits underneath it.
- ⛔ **No close-game measurement should be trusted until the seat asymmetry is explained.** The
  next diagnostic step is to find which tie-break in the engine or the AI is not mirror-invariant
  — the board, the seeding and the deploy rules have each been checked and are symmetric.
